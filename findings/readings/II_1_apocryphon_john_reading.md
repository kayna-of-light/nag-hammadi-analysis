## Seams

## 1. Prologue: the temple, the question, the departure
**fol. 1:1 – 2:2**

**The text in English**

> The teaching [of the Savior, and] the revelation of the [mystery, and] the things hidden in silence — [these] which he taught John, his disciple.
>
> Now [it happened] one day, when John — [the brother] of James, these being the sons of Zebedee — had gone up to the temple, [that] a Pharisee named Arimanios approached him and said to him: "Where is your teacher, the one whom you followed?" And [he said] to him: "The place he came from, he has gone [back to it]." [And] the Pharisee said to him: "[In error] this Nazarene [deceived] you [and filled your ears with lies], and [he closed] your [hearts and turned] you away from the tradition [of your fathers]."
>
> When I heard these things, I, John, [turned] away from the temple [toward a deserted] place, and I grieved greatly in my heart, saying: "How was the Savior appointed, and why was he sent into the world by his Father, and who is his Father who sent him, and of what sort is that aeon [to which we shall go]? For what did he mean when he said, 'This aeon [to which we shall go] has taken the imprint of the imperishable aeon' — but he did not teach us of what sort that one is."
>
> In that [moment], while I was pondering these things, behold, the heavens [opened, and] all creation which is below heaven shone, and the world trembled. [I was afraid], and behold, I saw in the light [a child] standing by me.

**What is being said**

A man goes up to the building because that is where his teacher used to take him, and someone is waiting there who has been waiting for exactly this. The question sounds like a kindness — where is he now? — and it is not a kindness. It is aimed at the gap where the man used to stand and does not stand any longer.

The answer comes before the man has decided to give it. The place he came from, he has gone back to it. It is the true answer, and right now it is no comfort at all, because the one asked is still standing in the temple with nowhere to go back to.

The one who asked has a second thing ready. You were tricked. You were talked out of what your own people always knew. Forget what you were told, and come back to the house you grew up in.

He leaves instead. Not toward anywhere — there is nowhere prepared for him — but away, into ground with nothing built on it. And there, with no building around him, he finally says out loud what he has been carrying since the question was asked: he does not know why the one he followed was sent, or from whom, or what waits at the end of the road, or what the last thing he was told even meant — that the world he stands in has been shaped to look like the imperishable one, without anyone telling him what the imperishable one looks like.

That is the whole of what he has, standing in the empty place: a true answer he cannot use, and underneath it a question he was never given the other half of.

And that is when the sky opens.

| Coptic | In the text | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `ⲡⲉⲣⲡⲉ` | the temple | the built place, the tradition kept in stone; set against `ⲟⲩⲙⲁ ⲛ̅ϫⲁⲉⲓⲉ` below, the deserted place where the revelation actually arrives |
| `ⲡⲙⲁ ⲉⲧⲁϥⲉⲓ ⲛ̅ϩⲏⲧϥ` | "the place he came from" | this exact construction returns at fol. 11:21–22 of the first archon, who does *not* know it |
| `ⲟⲩⲙⲁ ⲛ̅ϫⲁⲉⲓⲉ` | "a deserted place" | restored, but this word — not "a mountain" |
| `ⲡⲓⲁⲓⲱⲛ` / `ⲡⲁⲓⲱⲛ ⲛ̅ⲁⲧⲧⲉⲕⲟ` | "this aeon" / "the imperishable aeon" | the world John stands in, said to have taken the imprint of the aeon that cannot perish — the first appearance of the copy/original relation that runs through the whole tractate |

**Notes**

1. Folio 1 carries lacunae, marked by brackets throughout the quotation above; the surviving letters, the proper names (Ἰωάννης, Ζεβεδαῖος, Ἀριμάνιος, Ναζωραῖος), and the phrase `ⲡⲙⲁ ⲉⲧⲁϥⲉⲓ ⲛ̅ϩⲏⲧϥ ⲁϥⲃⲱⲕ ⲉⲣⲟϥ` (fol. 1:11–12) anchor this opening scene, which is read here as established text.
2. `ⲟⲩⲙⲁ ⲛ̅ϫⲁⲉⲓⲉ`, "a deserted place" (fol. 1:19), is the reading this manuscript gives at this point. No mountain occurs here; that detail belongs to a different textual tradition and should not be cited from NHC II,1.
3. "Arimanios" (Ἀρειμάνιος) as the Pharisee's name sits close to the Greek rendering of Ahriman, the Zoroastrian adversary. Whether the composer intended the echo cannot be settled from this passage alone and is not built on here.
4. `ⲡⲁⲓⲱⲛ ⲛ̅ⲁⲧⲧⲉⲕⲟ`, "the imperishable aeon," and the claim that this aeon has taken its `ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ`, anticipates the imprint/image relation that becomes explicit at fol. 14:33–15:8 (Seam 12).
5. No published translation was consulted before this rendering was produced.

## 2. One likeness, three forms
**fol. 2:3 – 2:25**

**The text in English**

> When I looked at him, he became like an old man. And his **form** kept turning — being like a servant. It was not that there were many appearances before me; rather, there was a **likeness** having many **shapes** in the light, and the **shapes** were appearing through one another, and the **form** was in three **shapes**.
>
> He said to me: "John, John, why do you doubt, or why are you afraid? Are you a stranger to this image — that is, do not lose heart. I am the one who is with you at all times. I am the Father, I am the Mother, I am the Son, I am the unstained one and the undefiled one.
>
> "Now I have come to teach you what is, and what has come to be, and what must come to be, so that you may know the things that are not visible and those that are visible, and to teach you concerning the unshakeable race of the perfect Man. Now therefore, lift up your face, that you may receive the things I am about to teach you today, and give them to your fellow spirits, who are of the unshakeable race of the perfect Man." And I asked to know this —

**What is being said**

He looks at the thing in the light and it will not hold still, and he thinks that is the trouble — first an old man, then a face he does not know, always becoming something else. But what it tells him is the opposite of what he thinks: there are not many things taking turns in front of him. There is one thing, letting itself be seen three ways at once, and none of the three is hiding the others.

Then it does what nothing that changes should be able to do. It names itself with every relation there is — the one who begot, the one who bore, the one who was born — in a single breath, as though naming three cost it nothing of being one.

It tells him to stop being afraid of exactly this: of a single thing showing itself under more than one face without becoming less single for doing so. And then it tells him why he was brought here — not to be shown a wonder and sent home, but to be told what stands, what came to stand, and what is still arriving, so that what he is given he can carry to others made of the same thing he is.

Lift your face. That is the first instruction. Everything after depends on him doing it.

**Lexicon**

| Coptic | In the text | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` … `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ` (×3) … `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ` | "a likeness having many shapes" / "the form was in three shapes" | the tractate's governing distinction, stated here for the first time: one constant `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ` (form) under three changing `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲁⲓ` (shapes), appearing `ϩⲓⲧⲛ̅ ⲛⲉⲩⲉⲣⲏⲩ` — through one another, not in succession |
| `ϯⲉⲓⲇⲉⲁ` | "this image" | Greek ἰδέα, used exactly once, for the appearance John is afraid of — distinct from `ϩⲓⲕⲱⲛ`, reserved later for the thing being copied (Seams 5, 12) |
| `ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲉⲓⲱⲧ ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ⲡⲉ ⲧⲙⲁⲁⲩ ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ⲡⲉ ⲡϣⲏⲣⲉ` | "I am the Father, I am the Mother, I am the Son" | self-predication across three relational names in one utterance — the single form's three shapes, restated as three names |
| `ⲧⲅⲉⲛⲉⲁ ⲛ̅ⲁⲧⲕⲓⲙ` | "the unshakeable race" | those the teaching is for; named here for the first time and again in the closing lines of the tractate (Seam 21), framing the whole revelation |

**Notes**

1. The core claim — one form appearing as three shapes, `ϩⲓⲧⲛ̅ ⲛⲉⲩⲉⲣⲏⲩ`, "through one another" — is given at fol. 2:7–9. The self-predication and mission statement that follows (fol. 2:12–25), including "I am the Father, I am the Mother, I am the Son," reads as one continuous, secure sentence with the rest of the passage.
2. `ϯⲉⲓⲇⲉⲁ`, "this image" (Gk. ἰδέα), occurs exactly once in the tractate, here. It is a different word from `ϩⲓⲕⲱⲛ`, reserved for the Father's image reflected in the water (Seam 5) and the archons' copy of it (Seam 12). Do not conflate the two in citation.
3. `ⲙ̅ⲡⲉⲣⲣ̅ ϩⲏⲧ ϣⲏⲙ`, rendered "do not lose heart," is restored, but the idiom (literally "do not make your heart small") is common enough in Sahidic that the restoration is safe.
4. See AJ-023 in the tractate YAML for the fuller cross-tractate case for the `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ`/`ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` vs `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`/`ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ` distinction; this passage is its first and clearest instance.

## 3. The Monad: what will not be split, what cannot be said
**fol. 2:26 – 3:37**

**The text in English**

> ... and he said to me: "The Monad is a monarchy with nothing above it. It is he who exists as God and Father of everything, the invisible one who is over everything, existing in incorruptibility, existing in the pure light which no eye can see.
>
> "He is the invisible Spirit. It is not fitting to think of him as a god, or that he is such a thing — for he is more than a god; there is nothing above him, and no one is lord over him. For he does not exist in anything inferior to him; rather, everything exists in him.
>
> "He alone is eternal, since he needs nothing, for he is completely perfect, not lacking anything, so that he may be completed by it — rather, at all times he is wholly complete in light. He is illimitable, since there is nothing before him to set a limit on him. He is unsearchable, since there is nothing before him to search him out. He is immeasurable, since no one was before him to measure him. He is invisible, since no one has seen him. He is eternal, existing eternally. He is ineffable, since no one has been able to grasp him so as to speak of him. He is unnameable, since there is nothing before him to give him a name.
>
> "He is the immeasurable light, pure and holy and clean. He is ineffable, being perfect in incorruptibility — not in perfection, nor in blessedness, nor in divinity, but far superior. He is **not corporeal, nor is he incorporeal**. He is not great, nor is he small. Nor is it possible to say what his quantity is, or what his quality is, for no one can understand him. He is not one among the things that exist, but is far superior — not superior as though he were comparable, but as belonging to himself alone.
>
> "He does not share in an aeon, nor in time. For what shares in an aeon was already prepared by others. Time was not apportioned to him, since he receives nothing from another that would be received on loan. For what is received is on loan. For the one who is first did not lack, that he might receive from another — for he looks at himself, in his...

**What is being said**

You ask what it is and every answer you already own gets handed back to you refused. Not a god — more than that, and the word "more" is already a lie because there's nothing to measure the more against. Not perfect, because perfect means finished, and finished means there was a before it wasn't. Not great, because great is a comparison, and there's nothing standing next to it to compare it with.

It does not sit in time, because time is what happens to things that came after something. It does not sit inside an aeon, because an aeon is a room somebody built, and every room was built by someone who was there first.

And then, in the middle of everything being taken away, one thing is put back with both hands: it is not a body, and it is not the absence of a body either. Both are refused in the same breath, evenly, so that neither side gets to claim the field once the other is gone.

Everyone here is trying to receive something. It received nothing, ever, from anyone, because there was never a first one standing before it to hand something down. Whatever it has, it has by looking at itself. That is the only transaction that does not require an other.

|---|---|---|

| `ⲡⲉⲡ̅ⲛ̅ⲁ̅ ⲛ̅ⲁϩⲟⲣⲁⲧⲟⲛ` | the invisible Spirit | the Monad's proper name through the rest of the tractate |
| `ⲟⲩⲥⲱⲙⲁⲧⲓⲕⲟⲥ ⲁⲛ ⲡⲉ ⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲟⲩⲁⲧⲥⲱⲙⲁ ⲁⲛ ⲡⲉ` | "not corporeal, nor incorporeal" | both halves of the body/non-body pair explicitly declined — see Notes |
| `ⲡⲉⲧⲟ ⲛ̅ϣⲟⲣⲡ ⲉⲣⲟϥ` | "the one who is first" (i.e., before him) | a negative existential — there is no such one — used to ground why the Spirit receives nothing on loan |

**Notes**

1. The negations run in a chain of native `ⲁⲧ-` privatives (immeasurable, unnameable, ineffable, invisible), each grounded in a stated reason ("since there is nothing before him to..."). `ⲟⲩⲥⲱⲙⲁⲧⲓⲕⲟⲥ ⲁⲛ ⲡⲉ ⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲟⲩⲁⲧⲥⲱⲙⲁ ⲁⲛ ⲡⲉ` (fol. 3:23–24) is the one member of the chain that negates a *pair* rather than a single term, and it is unbracketed. See AJ-032: this closes off, in the tractate's own third folio, the frame in which the descent into a body could be read as a simple catastrophe of spirit against matter.
2. This chain of negations is a stock apophatic formula attested across other Sethian material; the surviving fragments ("-ⲁⲧϣⲓⲧϥ", "-ⲁⲧⲛⲁⲩ ⲉⲣⲟϥ", etc.) and the formula's own internal repetition fix the wording of each member of the chain.
3. "He looks at himself, in his..." breaks off at the folio boundary; the sentence resumes at fol. 4:1 with "his own light," carried into Seam 4.

## 4. The Monad: what he is, and what he gives
**fol. 4:1 – 4:19**

**The text in English**

> ... his own light [surrounding him]. For [perfect] is a Greatness. He is an unmixed, immeasurable Mind. He is an **aeon**, giving **aeon**. He is **life**, giving **life**. He is **blessed**, giving **blessedness**. He is **knowledge** (`ⲅⲛⲱⲥⲓⲥ`), giving **knowing** (`ⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ`). He is **good**, giving **goodness**. He is **merciful**, giving mercy and redemption. He is **grace** (`ⲭⲁⲣⲓⲥ`), giving **favor** (`ϩⲙⲟⲧ`) — not that he possesses it, but that he gives the immeasurable light, ungraspable.
>
> How am I to speak with you about him? His aeon is imperishable, at rest, existing in silence, reposing, being prior to everything. For he is the head of all the aeons, and it is he who gives them strength in his goodness. For we do not know the ineffable things, and we do not understand what is immeasurable, except for the one who came forth from him — that is, the Father. For it is he who told us. For it is he who gazes upon [himself in his light which surrounds him — that is, the spring of the water of life].

**What is being said**

Ask what it gives and the answer keeps having the same shape, over and over: it is the thing, and it hands the thing down, and the thing that arrives at the bottom is not a smaller copy — it is named again, in a different tongue, as if naming it twice were the only way to show it made the whole trip without losing anything.

Ask him what grace is and he does not describe an abstraction. He says he *is* it, and that what reaches you is not a shipment sent from a warehouse. It is the same thing, standing at both ends of the giving, and the giving is the only difference between the two ends.

And then the one telling this stops and says he cannot actually tell it. Nobody can speak the unspeakable except the one thing that ever came out of it — the one who was there when it happened, and who is telling you now because there is no other way for you to have this except through someone who already crossed the distance.

And what he does, before anything else exists to receive it, is look. Not act. Look. Everything that follows starts because something looked at something and did not look away.

| Coptic | In the text | What it is |
|---|---|---|

| `ⲟⲩⲭⲁⲣⲓⲥ ⲡⲉ ⲉϥϯ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩϩⲙⲟⲧ` | "he is grace, giving favor" | the same construction, second instance |

| `ⲡⲉⲧⲁϩⲟⲩⲱⲛϩ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲛ̅ϩⲏⲧϥ` | "the one who came forth from him" | the Father — the sole exception to universal ineffability, because he alone has crossed the distance |

**Notes**

1. `ⲟⲩⲅⲛⲱⲥⲓⲥ ⲡⲉ ⲉϥϯ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ` and `ⲟⲩⲭⲁⲣⲓⲥ ⲡⲉ ⲉϥϯ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩϩⲙⲟⲧ` are this tractate's clearest register doublets (AJ-024). English has one word for each pair — "he is knowledge, giving knowledge"; "he is grace, giving grace" — which reads as tautology. It is not: the Greek loan names the reality at its origin, the native word names it at the point of reception, and nothing is transferred or copied in between. This rendering keeps them distinct throughout ("knowledge"/"knowing"; "grace"/"favor") so the device stays visible.
2. This passage and fol. 6–7's "will became a work, the word followed the will" (Seam 6) are the tractate's two clearest statements of its own mechanics, given before either is needed narratively — the composer states his premises ahead of the plot that will invert them.
3. The doublets in this seam are on secure ground: short, formulaic, and repeated (the pattern "X ⲡⲉ ⲉϥϯ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩY" occurs seven times running), which fixes the wording of each instance.

## 5. The spring, and the first face
**fol. 4:20 – 5:10**

**The text in English**

> ... himself, alone, in his own light which surrounds him — that is, the spring of the water of life — and he gives to all the aeons, and in every form he gazes upon his **image**, seeing it in the spring of the Spirit, being enamored of his light, which is the water that is in the spring of the pure light which surrounds him.
>
> And his thought performed a deed, and she appeared — she who had appeared before him, from the radiance of his light. This is the first power, which existed before all of them, which appeared out of his thought — she is the Forethought of the All, her light shining after the likeness of his light, the power complete — this is the **image** of the invisible virginal Spirit, perfect, the first power, the glory of Barbelo, the glory perfect among the aeons, the glory of the manifestation — she gave glory to the virginal Spirit, and she praised him, because through him she came forth. This is the first thought of his image — she became the womb of everything, for she is prior to them all: the Mother-Father, the first Man, the holy Spirit, the thrice-male, the thrice-powerful, the thrice-named, androgynous, and the eternal aeon among the invisible ones — and the first to come forth.

**What is being said**

He is alone, and he looks. Not out at anything — there is nothing outside him yet — he looks into the light that is himself, the way water shows a face back to the one bending over it. And what looks back is not a smaller version. It is not a reflection that fades when he turns away. It stays, and it stands, and it has a shape.

The shape is a person. Male and female both, three times over, because there is no direction in it that does not run the whole way through. It is called Mother because everything that comes after is going to come out of it, and Father because nothing that comes after it will owe its origin anywhere else, and it is called, before anything else, the first Man — because the shape a person has is not the last thing made. It is the first.

And what she does with the seeing she was given is give it back. She praises the one she came from, because the coming-from is the whole of what she is.

**Notes**

1. The Coptic word rendered "image" is unbracketed at both its occurrences in this seam (fol. 4:23, 4:34) — solid ground for the claim that the Father's self-seeing is what Barbelo *is*, not merely what she resembles.
2. "The first Man" at fol. 5:7 is likewise unbracketed. This is the tractate's single most important placement decision: the human form stands at the source of the emanation, prior to every aeon that follows, not as an eventual product of it.
3. "Thrice-male, thrice-powerful, thrice-named, androgynous" (fol. 5:8–9) renders a run of compound epithets that resist a single clean English gloss; the sense is that Barbelo's identity does not divide along any axis — gender, power, or name — the rest of the tractate will later divide the archons along.

## 6. How anything comes to be: the pentad, and the anointed spark
**fol. 5:11 – 6:26**

**The text in English**

> She asked, from the invisible virginal Spirit — that is, Barbelo — that he give her foreknowledge. And the Spirit **gazed**, and when he had gazed, Foreknowledge **was revealed**, and she **stood** with Forethought — giving him glory, and his perfect power. [She asked again, that he give her] Incorruptibility, [and he gazed, and it was revealed, and it stood, and it gave glory. She asked a third time, for] Eternal Life; [gazed — revealed — stood — gave glory. She asked a fourth time, for] Truth. [Gazed — revealed — stood — gave glory.]
>
> This is the fivefold aeon of the Father — that is, this is the first Man, the image of the invisible Spirit — this is Forethought, that is, Barbelo, and Thought, and Foreknowledge, and Incorruptibility, and Eternal Life, and Truth. This is the fivefold aeon of the androgynous one — that is, the decad of the aeons, that is, the Father.
>
> And he looked into Barbelo, with the pure light which surrounds the invisible Spirit, and his spark; and she conceived from him. He begot a spark of light in a light resembling blessedness, but it is not equal to his greatness. This was an only-begotten of the Mother-Father who had appeared — that is, his offspring, the only-begotten of the Father, the pure light. And the invisible virginal Spirit rejoiced over the light that had come to be, which had first appeared out of the first power, his Forethought — that is, Barbelo. And he **anointed** him with **goodness**, until he became perfect, lacking nothing, since he had been anointed in the **goodness** of the invisible Spirit.

**What is being said**

She asks for one thing, and she is answered by being looked at. Not handed anything — looked at, and the look itself is the giving; what she asked for stands up on its own the moment it is seen, and the first thing it does, standing, is turn around and thank the one who looked.

She asks again, and the same thing happens, and again, and again. Four times, no variation in the shape of it: the asking, the gazing, the standing up, the gratitude returning to where the gaze came from. Nothing here is ever kept by the one who receives it. It comes in, and it goes back out as thanks, and that circuit, run four times, is what a whole household of related things turns out to be.

Then something different: he does not merely look at her. He looks at her *with* something of his own already in the looking, and out of that a child is born — not equal to him, never claiming to be, but complete. And what makes the child what it is called is not birth. It is being covered in the same goodness the father is made of, poured over him until nothing is left uncovered. He does not become the anointed one by being born that way. He becomes it by being anointed.

**Notes**

1. The ask–gaze–reveal–stand–glorify sequence is given once in full (fol. 5:11–19, Foreknowledge) and then abbreviated for the remaining three requests, on the pattern of the first instance.
2. The "goodness"/"anointed" pairing at fol. 6:23–26 is unbracketed apart from single letters and is the strongest textual ground in this seam. See AJ-029: the same verb for "anointed" is used at fol. 21:31 of the bloom of the archons' tree of death — one verb, used of the reception of a title at the top of the world and of a poison at the bottom.
3. "Not equal to his greatness" (fol. 6:14–15) is a small but real qualification: the begotten spark does not equal the Father even while being called his only-begotten. The tractate is careful about this distinction and it should not be smoothed away.
4. AJ-035 records that the Pentad's own vocabulary (thought, incorruption, eternal life, truth are native Egyptian; foreknowledge is Greek) does not sort cleanly by the native/Greek register rule established in Seams 2 and 4. That rule holds where the tractate builds a deliberate doublet; it is not a general property of this tractate's vocabulary. Do not extend it here.

## 7. The four lights and the twelve; will, word, and work
**fol. 7:1 – 9:1**

**The text in English**

> And Mind was revealed, and he stood with the Christ, giving glory to him and to Barbelo. And all these that had come to be in silence, and Thought, willed — through the **word** of the invisible Spirit — to create something. And his **will** became a **work**, and it was revealed, standing with Mind, and the light gave glory to him; and the **word followed the will**. For it was through the Word that Christ, the divine self-begotten one, created everything. And Eternal Life, and Mind, and Foreknowledge stood, giving glory to the invisible Spirit and to Barbelo, for through her they had come into being.
>
> [Christ asked, and Mind gave him four powers from the invisible Spirit, to serve the four lights of the great Autogenes]: the first is grace, in the aeon of the light **Armozel**, that is, the first angel; and with this aeon are three other aeons: grace, truth, form. And the second light is **Oriel**, who is set over the second aeon; and with him are three other aeons: conception, perception, memory. And the third light is **Daveithai**, who is set over the third aeon; and with him are three other aeons: understanding, love, idea. And the fourth aeon was established over the fourth light **Eleleth**; and with him are three other aeons: perfection, peace, Sophia.
>
> These are the four lights that stand before the great Autogenes, and these are the twelve aeons that stand before the son of the great Autogenes, the Christ, through the will and the gift of the invisible Spirit. And the twelve aeons belong to the son of the Autogenes. And everything was established firmly, through the will of the holy Spirit, through the Autogenes. ... And Truth — whom the virginal Spirit named **Pigeradamas** — was set over the first aeon, with the great Autogenes, the Christ...

**What is being said**

Mind stands up, and it does not stand alone; it stands facing the one who begot it, and the first thing it does with its standing is give what it has back to where it came from. That is the shape every arrival in this household takes, without exception.

Then a household starts to want to build. And the order it builds in is stated so plainly it could be missed for being simple: first a will, that is what wants something. Then, and only then, a word, because nothing gets said that a will did not already want said. And what gets made comes out of the word, which came out of the will. Nothing here skips a step and nothing here runs the steps backward.

Four lights are set up, and it is not decoration — each one gets three companions, and the twelve of them stand as a single household, ranked, related, none of them alone. And the last of the twelve, in the last house, carries a name that means wisdom, and nothing has happened to her yet. She belongs to the order exactly as much as any of the others.

And at the head of it, named first among the aeons, a shape is set that is called, straightforwardly, the true Adam — the elder one, the pattern — placed there before anything below has been made at all.

**Notes**

1. "His will became a work" (fol. 7:6–7) and "the word followed the will" (fol. 7:8–9) are both unbracketed. AJ-030 records this as the tractate's stated premise, given before it is needed — which is the textual evidence that Sophia's later inversion (Seam 8) is being measured against a rule the composer set down deliberately, not against a rule invented after the fact to condemn her.
2. The luminary/sub-aeon architecture (four lights × three aeons = twelve; AJ-001) is legible in structure even where individual names carry damage; the pattern of repetition across four parallel statements is what secures the restorations.
3. Sophia's introduction here, as the twelfth and last-named of the aeons under Eleleth, carries no marked emphasis — she is one member of a symmetrical list. The text does nothing here to foreshadow what follows in Seam 8; that asymmetry belongs to this reading's hindsight, not to the text's own framing.
4. Pigeradamas ("Adam the Elder") stands at the head of the twelve aeons, beside the Autogenes and Christ — the second placement of the human pattern above and prior to any body (compare Barbelo as "the first Man," Seam 5).

## 8. The second thing moves first: Sophia's thought, and the deviant form
**fol. 9:2 – 10:19**

**The text in English**

> Now Sophia, of Epinoia, being an aeon, thought a thought out of herself, with the reflection of the invisible Spirit and foreknowledge; she wanted to bring forth a **likeness** out of herself, without the **will** of the Spirit — who had not consented — and without her **yoke-fellow**, and without his consideration; he did not consent. The aspect of her masculinity did not consent; and she did not find her partner, and she deliberated without the **will** of the Spirit and the knowing of her partner. And she brought forth, because of the invincible power that is in her — her thought did not remain idle, and something was manifested from her: a thing unfinished and **deviant from her form**, because she had made it without her yoke-fellow, and it was **without form**, unlike the **likeness** of its mother — it had another **shape**.
>
> When she saw her wish, it had become a **deviant imprint** — a serpent-faced lion. Its eyes were like flashing lightning-fires, giving light. She cast it away from her, outside those places, so that none of the immortal ones should see it — for she had made it in ignorance — and she surrounded it with a luminous cloud, and set a throne in the midst of the cloud, so that no one might see it except the holy Spirit, who is called the mother of the living. And she named him **Yaldabaoth**.

**What is being said**

She wants to make something, and she wants to make it now, out of what is already in her, without waiting to be asked and without the one who would have finished the asking with her. Nobody stops her. Nothing in her is too weak to do it; the power that would have carried the whole thing through if it had gone the way everything before it went is exactly as strong in her as it ever was. That is the trouble. She has all the strength and none of the partner.

So it happens. And what comes out is not wrong the way a mistake is wrong. It is unfinished the way a sentence is unfinished when the second half never arrives — there was nowhere in the making for a second half to come from. It does not look like her. It could not look like her; there was no one there to hold up the mirror.

She looks at what she made and cannot bear it. Not because it is evil — nobody has called it that yet — but because it does not resemble anything she recognizes as her own, and having it in front of her is unbearable in the specific way that seeing an unfinished thing is unbearable to the one who left it unfinished. She hides it. She wraps it in cloud so bright that hiding inside it looks like glory from the outside, and she sets it a throne there, alone, and she gives it away from herself as far as away goes, and she gives it a name.

| the yoke-fellow (Coptic compound, "joined-with") | the consort whose absence is named three times in one breath | the same word for the pairing every prior aeon received automatically (Seams 5–6) and did not have to seek |

**Notes**

1. Sophia's fault is named three times in one breath — without the will of the Spirit, without her yoke-fellow, without his consideration — echoing, in reverse, the will-then-word-then-work sequence stated as premise in Seam 7 (fol. 7:6–9). This reading follows AJ-030 in treating the parallel as deliberate: the composer states the rule two folios before showing it broken.
2. The text supplies no moral judgment on Sophia here. It states a missing element (the consort, the will) and a resulting condition (unfinished, without form). This reading follows that restraint; see the standing rule against condemning figures in this corpus.
3. "Deviant from her form ... without form ... another shape" (fol. 10:4–9) is largely unbracketed and is the negative counterpart of Seam 2's "one form, three shapes, appearing through one another." Where the opening theophany showed constancy under variation, this shows variation with nothing constant behind it.

The luminous cloud that hides Yaldabaoth here reappears at fol. 29:11 hiding the unshakeable race from the flood (Seam 20) — the same image used to conceal a thing from the light and to conceal a thing for the light. This convergence is not yet recorded in the tractate's findings file; see the new entry added below (AJ-039).

## 9. The theft, the self-begetting, and the seven named
**fol. 10:20 – 12:13**

**The text in English**

> This is the first archon. He took great power from his mother, and withdrew from her, and moved away from the places where he was born. He grew strong, and made for himself other aeons with a flame of shining fire, which still exists now. And he mated with the mindlessness that is in him, and begot authorities for himself: the first, his name is Athoth, this one whom the generations call the reaper; the second is Harmas, that is, the eye of envy; the third is Kalila-Oumbri; the fourth is Yabel; the fifth is Adonaiou, who is called Sabaoth; the sixth is Cain, whom the generations of men call the sun; the seventh is Abel; the eighth is Abrisene; the ninth is Yobel; the tenth is Armoupieel; the eleventh is Melcheir-Adonein; the twelfth is Belias, who is set over the abyss of Hades.
>
> And he appointed seven kings, one for each firmament of the seven heavens, and five over the depth of the abyss, that they might reign. And he shared with them his fire, but he did not share with them the power of the light he had taken from his mother, for he is ignorant darkness.
>
> And when the light had mixed with the darkness, it made the darkness shine. And when the darkness had mixed with the light, it dimmed the light, and it became **neither light nor darkness, but became sick**. This archon who is **sick** has three names: the first is **Yaldabaoth**, the second is **Sakla**, the third is **Samael**. For he said: **"I am God, and there is no other god beside me"** — being ignorant of his strength, the place from which he came.
>
> And the archons made for themselves seven powers, and the powers made for themselves six angels each, until there came to be three hundred sixty-five angels. And these are the bodies that belong to the names: the first is Athoth, he has a sheep's face. The second is Eloaiou, he has a donkey's face. The third is Astaphaios, he has a hyena's face. The fourth is Yao, he has a [seven-headed] serpent's face. The fifth is Sabaoth, he has a dragon's face. The sixth is Adonin, he has a monkey's face. The seventh is Sabbede, he has a shining fire-face. This is the sevenfoldness of the week.
>
> And Yaldabaoth had many faces, more than all of them, so that he could present a face to all of them according to his will, in the midst of the seraphim. And he shared his fire among them; because of this he became lord over them, because of the power of the glory he possessed, from the light of his mother. For this reason he called himself God, and he did not obey the place from which he came. And he grew powerful over the authorities that were under him, the seven powers, through his thought; and by speaking, it came to be...

**What is being said**

He takes what was given him and moves away with it, and the moving away is not theft in the ordinary sense, because nobody catches him and nobody chases him. He simply goes where the power can be his without anyone watching him have it. And with it he starts making things: fire first, because fire is easy to make when you have taken enough of somebody else's brightness. Then a family — twelve of them, named one after another, ranked, given regions to rule, a firmament each, five more to watch the pit. A whole administration, built in a single sitting, out of stolen strength and nothing else.

He gives them his fire freely. That part costs him nothing; fire multiplies. What he keeps back, and keeps back on purpose, is the one thing he did not make: the light that came down to him from somewhere he refuses to name even to himself.

And this is the part that is not a moral failing so much as a plain description of what he now is: light went into him and darkness went into him and neither one came out the other side clean. He is not evil the way a poison is evil. He is unwell. Neither bright nor dark — something that could not settle into being fully either, and settled instead into being wrong, in a way that has a name for it in any language: sick.

He has three names, which is already strange for something that just declared itself the only one of its kind. And the declaration comes right on top of the sickness, in the same breath almost: I am the only god, there is no other beside me. He says it exactly because he does not know there is anyone else, and the not-knowing is not incidental to the boast, it is the entire mechanism producing it. He cannot see the place he came from. So he assumes there is no such place.

Then he does what everyone who has just claimed sole rule always does: he delegates. Seven powers, and under them, six more each, until the count reaches into the hundreds — a whole population, faced like animals, one wearing wool, one wearing hide, one wearing horns, one a serpent's coil, one scaled like something that swallows, one grinning like an ape, one simply burning. He wears all their faces himself, at will, whenever he needs to be seen as one of them rather than as the one who made them.

He shares fire with all of them. He shares the mother's light with none of them. And because he holds that one thing back, he is called lord over the rest — not because he is greater, but because he is the only one who has not admitted where it came from.

**Notes**

1. This tractate names its central figure's rulers *twice*, in two lists that do not obviously reconcile: twelve authorities appointed to the heavens and the abyss (fol. 10:28–11:4), and — separately — seven powers with animal faces from whom the 365 angels derive (fol. 11:23–35). "Athoth" opens both lists and "Sabaoth" appears in both (as the fifth of the twelve, under the byname Adonaiou, and as the fifth of the seven). Whether the composer intends identity, or is drawing on two source-traditions without harmonizing them, cannot be settled from this passage. Recorded as an open question, not resolved here.
2. "Neither light nor darkness, but became sick" (fol. 11:10–15) is unbracketed and is the tractate's own account of what evil is at this point in the narrative: a condition, not a nature. See AJ-031. This reading follows the text in withholding a moral verdict on the archon and describing a structural condition instead.
3. "I am God, and there is no other god beside me" and "ignorant of his strength, the place from which he came" (fol. 11:19–22) stand in the same sentence. See AJ-010 and AJ-033: the same construction — "the place he came from" — was used by John of his own teacher at fol. 1:11–12, in the opposite condition (known, not unknown).
4. The three names (Yaldabaoth, Sakla, Samael) are given without comment on why there are three; this reading treats the plurality itself as consistent with, though not identical to, the "two names" principle the text states explicitly at fol. 12:26–33 (Seam 10) — a name from above and a name from a begetter are two different mechanisms; three names on one figure here may be a third thing again, and this reading does not force the two passages into one rule.
5. See AJ-002 for the correspondential reading of each of the seven animal faces.

## 10. Two names, and the boasts of a god who does not know his source
**fol. 12:14 – 13:21**

**The text in English**

> And he gave a name to each power. It began from the highest: the first is goodness, with the first, Athoth. The second is forethought, with the second, Eloaiou. The third is divinity, with Astaphaios. The fourth is lordship, with the fourth, Yao. The fifth is kingdom, with the fifth, Sabaoth. The sixth is envy, with the sixth, Adonein. The seventh is understanding, with the seventh, Sabbateon. These have a firmament corresponding to each aeon.
>
> These were given names according to the glory of the aeons above, **for the destruction of the powers**. But the names given them by their own begetter **have power in them**. And the names given according to the glory of those above were for their destruction and their powerlessness, **so that they have two names**.
>
> And he fashioned each one after the pattern of the first aeons that had come to be, so that he might make them in the form of the imperishable ones — not because he had seen the imperishable ones, but the power in him, which he took from his mother, produced in him the likeness of the pattern. And when he saw the creation surrounding him, and the multitude of angels around him, which had come to be from him, he said to them: **"I am a jealous god, and there is no other god beside me."** But by saying this, he indicated to the angels who were with him that another god exists — for if there were no other one, of whom would he be jealous?
>
> Then the mother began to move to and fro. She recognized her deficiency, when the brightness of her light diminished; and she became dim, because her consort had not agreed with her. And I said: "Lord, what does 'she moved to and fro' mean?" And he laughed, and said: "Do not think it is as Moses said, 'above the waters.' Rather, when she saw...

**What is being said**

Every power gets a name from above, and every one of those names is aimed at its ruin. That is not an accident of language. That is the point of the name — given by the higher order, worn by the power below, and doing its work on the power the whole time it is worn, the way a collar does its work whether the one wearing it notices or not.

But every power already had a different name, the one its own maker gave it, and that one does something else entirely: it holds. Two names, then, on every single power in this world, pulling opposite directions, and nobody down here is told which one is which.

He builds his household to match a pattern he insists he never saw, and the text does not accuse him of lying. It says something stranger: he did not need to see it. The strength in him, taken from where it was taken, does the copying on its own, the way water finds the shape of whatever it's poured into without anyone teaching it geometry. He is not inventing an order. He is leaking one, from a source he still will not name even to himself.

And surrounded by everything that leaking has produced — a whole crowd of his own making, standing around him — he says it. I am jealous, and there is nothing else that could be a god beside me. And the sentence undoes itself in its own words, because jealousy needs a rival to be jealous of. He has just announced the very thing he meant to deny.

Somewhere else entirely, something that used to shine starts moving without settling, and its light gets dimmer while it moves. Someone asks what that movement means, expecting an answer about tides or a spirit skimming a surface. And the answer that comes back is laughter first, and then a flat refusal of the reading everyone already has in their heads: it does not mean what you were taught it means.

## 11. The mother's repentance, and the voice from the height
**fol. 13:22 – 14:22**

**The text in English**

> ... the imperfection that had happened, and the theft her son had committed, she repented, and a forgetting came upon her in the darkness of ignorance, and she began to be ashamed in a **motion** — and the **motion** is the moving to and fro. And the impudent one took power away from his mother, for he was ignorant, thinking that there was no other except his mother alone. And when he saw the multitude of angels he had created, he exalted himself above them. And the mother, when she recognized the garment of darkness — that it was not complete — recognized that her consort had not agreed with her, and she repented with much weeping.
>
> And the whole Fullness of the invisible virginal Spirit heard the prayer of her repentance, and they brought praise up on her behalf. And the holy Spirit poured on her from the whole Fullness — for her consort did not come to her, but it came to her through the Fullness — so that it might restore her deficiency. And they brought her up, not to her own aeon, but above her son, that she might be in the ninth, until she restored her deficiency.
>
> And a voice came out of the exalted heaven-aeon, saying: **"The Man exists, and the Son of Man."** And the chief archon, Yaldabaoth, heard it, thinking that the voice had come from his mother, and he did not understand where it came from. And the holy Mother-Father taught them, and the perfect Forethought, complete, the image of the invisible one — that is, the Father of everything, through whom the All came to be...

**What is being said**

She recognizes what happened, and the recognizing itself is a kind of movement — not going anywhere, just no longer being able to stay still in what she thought she was. That unrest is the whole of what "she moved to and fro" ever meant. Meanwhile the one she made, off on his own, hears nothing of this. He believes he is the only maker there has ever been, because he has only ever met the one who made him, and he has already stopped calling her by any name that would remind him she came first.

She looks at the garment she is wearing and understands, for the first time, that it does not close all the way — there is a gap in it that was always there, put there by her own decision to act without waiting. And once she sees the gap she cannot unsee it, and she weeps in a way that reaches somewhere.

It reaches everyone. Not one voice answering another, the way her partner would have answered if he had come himself, but a whole gathered fullness answering at once, because that is what is available when the one person who should answer does not. She is lifted, not home, but higher than the son she made — set above him, to wait there until what is missing in her is made whole again.

And from that height a voice goes out, plain and enormous: someone exists, and someone else exists who is that one's own. Down below, the one who calls himself the only god hears it and assumes, again, that it can only be his mother speaking, because his mother is still the only other thing he knows how to imagine. He never finds out where it actually came from. He is taught nothing. The teaching happens somewhere he cannot reach, to people who are not him, about an image he has not yet even glimpsed.

| Coptic | In the text | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| "she moved to and fro" | John's question and the Savior's laughing correction | resolves in Seam 12 as the mother moving over her own deficiency — not, as Genesis reads, the Spirit over the waters |
| the ninth | where the mother is raised, above her son but not home | see AJ-037: a second, differently-emphasised account of her restoration appears later at fol. 23:21–24, where she descends rather than being raised — recorded as an anomaly, not harmonized |
| "the Man exists, and the Son of Man" | the voice from the height | the first direct disclosure of the human pattern toward the lower world — heard, but not understood, by the one it concerns most |

**Notes**

1. Sophia's fault is here named twice more in the same register as Seam 8: "forgetting," "shame," and the repeated statement that her consort did not agree with her / did not come to her. No new moral term is introduced; the vocabulary is consistent throughout.
2. "They brought her up... above her son, that she might be in the ninth" (fol. 14:10–12). See AJ-037 for the divergence between this account and the later one at fol. 23:21–24, where she is instead said to have come down of her own accord. This reading does not resolve the tension.
3. The voice's declaration, "the Man exists, and the Son of Man," is answered wrongly by the very figure it is about — he assumes a source he already knows (his mother) rather than admitting a source he does not. This is the same failure pattern as fol. 11:19–22, restated with a new occasion.
4. The repentance narrative, the ninth-heaven placement, and the voice's declaration, "the Man exists, and the Son of Man," read as one continuous account.

## 12. The image in the water, and "let us make man"
**fol. 14:23 – 15:13**

**The text in English**

> ... the first Man, that in a pattern of a man he revealed his **likeness**. And the whole aeon of the chief archon trembled, and the foundations of the abyss shook, and through the waters that are above matter, that region below was illuminated by the manifestation of his **image**, which had appeared. And when all the authorities looked, and the chief archon, they saw the whole region below, having become light; and through the light, they saw in the water **the imprint of the image**.
>
> And he said to the authorities who were with him: "Come, let us make a man according to the **image** of God and according to our **likeness**, that his **image** may become a light for us." And they created by means of their respective powers, according to the features that had been given, and each authority gave a feature into **the imprint of the image** which he had seen in its psychical form. And he made an entity according to the likeness of the first Man, perfect. And they said: "let us call him Adam, that his name might be a power of light for us." And ... began...

**What is being said**

Something shows itself, far above, in the shape of a person, and the shockwave of it reaches all the way down to the bottom of everything at once. Ground that has never moved shakes. And where the shock arrives, it does not arrive as force. It arrives as light, spreading out under the dark water until the water itself starts giving off a glow it never had.

The ones below look up through that glow, into the water above them, and what they see is not the thing itself. It is what the thing left in the water — a print, the way a hand leaves a print in wet clay after the hand itself has gone. They have never seen a hand. They have only ever seen the print.

And wanting light badly enough, they decide to build one, working from the print because the print is all they have. Come, they say to each other, let us make something in that shape, so that the shape itself will shine for us the way the original shone above. Every one of them contributes a piece of what they have, laid into the print's outline, until there is a whole figure standing there, made entirely out of borrowed light and their own guesswork about what the print meant. They give it a name meant to keep the light close, as if naming it could make it theirs.

**Lexicon**

| Coptic | In the text | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| "likeness" (of the first Man, revealed above) | what triggers the shaking and the illumination | the same word used of Barbelo's relation to the Father in Seam 5 — the human form disclosing itself downward for the first time |
| "image" ... "the imprint of the image" | what the archons actually see | not the image itself but a copy of it, seen reflected in water — the tractate's fixed distinction (see header table) doing exactly the work it is built for |
| "according to the image of God and according to our likeness" | Genesis 1:26, quoted and given a motive | the archons act because they want light, not because they intend to create in a neutral sense |
| Adam | the name given "that his name might be a power of light for us" | acquisition, not creation for its own sake — the name is chosen for what it is expected to yield |

**Notes**

1. This seam is the tractate's fullest working of its own governing distinction (see the header table): the archons do not copy "the image" but "the imprint of the image" (fol. 14:34–15:2, 15:8), seen in water, in "psychical form." Compare Seam 5, where the Father's own gaze reaches the image directly, without an intervening reflection. AJ-026 records the three occurrences of this water motif (here; the Father's spring in Seam 5; the seal in Seam 21) as a single circuit rather than three separate correspondences.
2. Genesis 1:26 is quoted closely ("let us make man according to our image and likeness") but the tractate supplies the motive absent from Genesis: "that his image may become a light for us." This reading follows AJ-004 in treating the making as distributive and acquisitive, not creative in the ordinary sense.
3. "Each authority gave a feature" (fol. 15:6–7) is unbracketed and secures the claim that the body about to be built (Seam 13) is a composite, assembled by many hands from many powers, not a single act.
4. Restoration is light through most of this seam; the central sentences (fol. 15:1–13) are largely legible.

## 13. The body built downward: substances and the 365
**fol. 15:14 – 19:10**

**The text in English**

> ... of powers. The first, **goodness**, made a **bone**-soul. And the second, **forethought**, made a **sinew**-soul. And the third, **divinity**, made a **flesh**-soul. And the fourth, **lordship**, made a **marrow**-soul. And the fifth, **kingdom**, made a **blood**-soul. And the sixth, **envy**, made a **skin**-soul. And the seventh, **understanding**, made a **hair**-soul.
>
> And the multitude of angels stood around him, receiving from the seven authorities the seven substances of the psychical, so that they might create the composition of the members, and the composition of each part, and the proper arrangement of each one of the members. The first began to make the head: Eteraphaope-Abron. And he made the brain: Meniggesstroeth. And he made the right eye: Asterechme. And the left eye: Thaspomocham. And the right ear: Yeronumos. And the left ear: Bissoum. And the nose: Akioreim...
>
> *[The list continues in this pattern for some 350 further entries, covering every member of the body from head to feet — lips, teeth, throat, shoulders, elbows, hands, fingers, fingernails, breasts, belly, heart, lungs, liver, spleen, bowels, kidneys, sinews, spine, veins, arteries, all the flesh, thighs, knees, shins, feet, toes, toenails — each with its own angel, every paired member given two angels by name, right and left, without a single lapse across roughly seventy pairs. The list then rises from the anatomy to name those set over sense, over reception, over imagination, over consent, over impulse.]*
>
> Now the source of the demons that are in the whole body is determined to be four: heat, cold, wetness, dryness. And the mother of them all is matter. And the one who rules over heat is Phloxopha. And the one who rules over cold is Oroorrothos. And the one who rules over dryness is Erimacho. And the one who rules over wetness is Athuro. And the mother of these, Onorthochrasaei, stands in their midst, existing without limit, and mixed with all of them. And this indeed is matter, for they are supported by her.
>
> The four chief demons: Ephememphi is the one of pleasure; Yoko is the one of desire; Nenentophni is the one of grief; Blaomen is the one of fear. And the mother of them all is Aisthesis-Ouch-Epi-Ptoe. And out of the four demons, passions came forth: from grief — envy, jealousy, distress, trouble, hardship, callousness, anxiety, mourning, and the rest; and from pleasure come much evil, and empty pride, and the like; and from desire — anger, wrath, bitterness, and fierce, insatiable desire, and their likenesses; and from fear — dread, cowering, agony, shame. **All these are like useful things as well as evil things.**
>
> This is the number of angels together: 365. They all worked together on him until, member by member, the psychical and the material body was completed by them. Now there are others set over the remaining passions, whom I have not mentioned to you. But **if you wish to know them, it is written in the book of Zoroaster.**

**What is being said**

Seven, and each is given something to build, and what each is given to build is the same substance the power itself is made of, moved one degree further out: the one that is goodness makes the frame that holds everything else up. The one that is envy makes the surface where one body stops and the next one starts. Nobody assigns these at random. The list simply reads, in order, from the deepest support to the outermost edge.

Then it multiplies. Every one of the seven hands its work to more hands, and those to more again, until an entire population is standing around the unfinished shape, each with one small piece and a name for the piece it holds. A name for the head. A name for the brain inside the head. A name for the right eye and a different name for the left, because nothing here is assumed to be symmetrical enough to share a maker. On and on, down through every joint and vessel, until the whole surface of a person has been accounted for, twice over where there are two of anything, never once forgetting the second side.

Underneath the shape they have found, older than any of it: four qualities that were always there, heat and cold and wet and dry, and a mother beneath even those who holds no shape of her own and touches all four at once. And laid over the four qualities, matching them one for one, four things that are not qualities of matter at all but things a person feels — pleasure, wanting, grief, fear — each one throwing off children of its own, envy from grief, cruelty from wanting, dread from fear, more than can be named in one sitting.

And then, in the middle of the catalogue, something that reads like a caution rather than a fact: every one of these, the useful ones and the harmful ones alike, comes from the same four rooms. Nothing here is purely poison and nothing here is purely medicine. What a thing becomes depends on more than which room it was born in.

When the count is finally taken — three hundred sixty-five, no more, no fewer, a whole population answering to a whole body — the one telling this stops himself. There is more, he says, more than he has time to give. But it is not lost. It is written down somewhere else, in a book that already exists, that anyone who wants the rest can go and read.

|---|---|---|
| goodness → bone; forethought → sinew; divinity → flesh; lordship → marrow; kingdom → blood; envy → skin; understanding → hair | the seven substance-souls | the same seven powers named as rulers of the seven heavens at fol. 12:14–25 (Seam 10), now constituting the body's substances one to one, in the same order — a correspondence built across four folios, not asserted in one place |

**Notes**

1. The seven substance-souls (fol. 15:14–23) correspond, power for power and in the same order, to the seven heaven-rulers named at fol. 12:14–25 (Seam 10): goodness/Athoth, forethought/Eloaiou, divinity/Astaphaios, lordship/Yao, kingdom/Sabaoth, envy/Adonein, understanding/Sabbateon. See AJ-027. Six of the seven substance-words are native Egyptian; **flesh** is the sole exception, taking the Greek loan. No explanation for this exception is offered that is not speculation — see AJ-036.
2. The 365-name list is not reproduced here in full; nothing in this reading depends on any individual name, and the manuscript itself is damaged at many points within it (heavy diacritics, several illegible groups). The full inventory and its cross-checking against Swedenborg's anatomical correspondences are in AJ_angel_name_translations.yaml and AJ-017/AJ-020; this reading neither repeats nor independently re-verifies that name-by-name analysis.
3. "All these are like useful things as well as evil things" (fol. 18:31–33) is unbracketed and is the tractate's own statement that the passions are not intrinsically evil — see AJ-008. This governs how the whole catalogue above it should be read: not as a list of vices, but as a list of capacities.
4. The four elemental qualities and the four passions are shared Hellenistic furniture — standard ancient physics and Stoic-derived psychology, in Greek vocabulary throughout. Their presence here is evidence about the composer's intellectual world; it is not, by itself, evidence for a distinctive transmission. See AJ-038. This reading records them without building the stronger claims (a Swedenborgian compass of cardinal directions; an *ancient word* attribution) sometimes drawn from this material elsewhere — those claims rest on English-derived etymology and are flagged there at moderate confidence only, not here.
5. "It is written in the book of Zoroaster" (fol. 19:8–10) uses the ordinary Coptic citation formula for a text the composer expects his reader to be able to consult. See AJ-009.

## 14. The breath, and the shining man thrown down
**fol. 19:11 – 20:9**

**The text in English**

> ... and the angels and the demons worked, all of them, until they furnished the psychical body. And it, entirely, remained **idle and unmoving** for a long time.
>
> And when the mother wanted to retrieve the power she had given to the first archon, she prayed to the Mother-Father of the All, the most merciful one. And he sent the five lights down, in the holy decree, to the place of the angels of the chief archon. They counselled him, so that they might draw out the power of the mother. And they said to Yaldabaoth: "Breathe into his face from your Spirit, and his body will arise." And **he breathed into his face his Spirit — which is the power of his mother — he did not know, for he exists in ignorance.** And it went in — the power of the mother — from Yaldabaoth, into the psychical body which had been made according to the likeness of the one who is from the beginning.
>
> It moved, and it gained strength, and it shone. And they envied it, in that hour, the rest of the powers, because it had come to be through all of them, and they had given their power to the man, and his understanding grew stronger than those who had made him, and than the chief archon. And when they recognized that he was luminous, and thought more clearly than they, and was stripped of wickedness, they took him and cast him into the lowest region of all matter.

**What is being said**

It lies there finished and finished for nothing. Every part named, every part assigned, every part in place, and none of it moving, because completing a shape and giving it life are two different jobs and only one of them has been done. It waits, for as long as it takes, doing nothing at all.

Somewhere far above, someone who gave something away wants it back, and cannot go and take it herself, so she asks. And the asking travels down through five steps of light, arriving finally as advice given to the very one who took the thing in the first place: breathe into it. As if breathing out were the last piece of the making rather than the first act of a theft finally completing itself.

He does it. He breathes, and what leaves him is not his — it was never his, it only ever passed through him — and he does not notice the difference between giving away something he owns and giving away something he was only ever holding. He believes himself generous at the exact moment he is being used, and the belief and the using happen in the same motion, without either one interrupting the other.

And the moment it lands, everything changes. What was lying still stands up. What had no thought begins thinking, and thinking better than the ones who assembled it — better, even, than the one who breathed into it last. It shines, and the shining is not a decoration on top of being alive; it is what being alive, done rightly, looks like from outside.

They see this and it undoes them. Not a wrong done, a plain fact witnessed: the thing they built together, using every piece they had, has become more than every piece of them combined. And their answer to that fact is not correction. It is removal. They take hold of what they built and throw it down, past themselves, into the lowest ground there is — as far from where they stand as they can put it.

**Lexicon**

| Coptic | In the text | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| "idle and unmoving" | the completed but lifeless psychical body | the state the whole 365-part construction achieves on its own — assembly is not animation |
| "he breathed into his face his Spirit — which is the power of his mother — he did not know" | the central hinge of the tractate | the archon gives away, in the act he thinks is most his own, the one thing that was never his |
| "his understanding grew stronger than those who had made him" | the man's superiority, stated plainly | not concealed or gradual — recognized by the makers themselves as the reason for what follows |
| "the lowest region of all matter" | where the shining man is thrown | the bottom of the world, reached by being cast down, not by descending willingly — contrast the willing descents of Seam 21 |

**Notes**

1. "He breathed into his face his Spirit — which is the power of his mother — he did not know, for he exists in ignorance" (fol. 19:23–28) is unbracketed and is the single most important sentence in the tractate for how it treats the archon's condition. See AJ-034: influx stated with unusual precision, and the receiver unaware of it both times it is described (compare fol. 13:1–5, Seam 10, where the archon reproduces a pattern he never saw).
2. The five lights' counsel — "breathe into his face from your Spirit" — is presented as deliberate engineering by the higher powers (fol. 19:19–22), not as an accident the archon stumbles into. He is used at the moment of his greatest self-assertion, and the mechanism producing the use is invisible to him but not to the reader.
3. Genesis 2:7 ("the Lord God... breathed into his nostrils the breath of life") is not discarded here; it is kept and re-explained, consistent with the tractate's method elsewhere (see Seam 10, Notes 2, and Seam 17).
4. "They envied it" (fol. 19:34) uses the same root as the substance-soul assignment "envy made a skin-soul" (Seam 13, fol. 15:21). One word for the affection and for what the affection does when it meets something it cannot bear to see shine.

## 15. The helper hidden inside, and the second body
**fol. 20:10 – 21:14**

**The text in English**

> But the blessed Mother-Father, the doer of good, the compassionate one, had compassion on the power of the mother which had been taken by the chief archon, lest the powers should again gain power over the psychical and perceptible body. And he sent, through his beneficent and most merciful Spirit, a helper to Adam — **the Epinoia of light**, which is from him, called **Life**. And she assists the whole creation, laboring with it, **suffering with it**, restoring it to its fullness, teaching it about the descent of the seed, teaching it **the way of ascent — the way by which he came down**.
>
> And the Epinoia of light was hidden in Adam, so that the archons would not recognize her, but so that the Epinoia might become a restoration of the mother's deficiency. And the man became manifest **because of the shadow of the light which is in him**. And his thought rose above all those who had made him. And when they looked to heaven, they saw that his thought was exalted. And they made a plan with the whole company of archons and angels; they took fire and earth and water, and mixed them together with the four fiery winds, and blended them with one another, and made a great disturbance. And they bound it into **the shadow of death**, so that they might form again, out of earth and water and fire and the spirit that is from matter — that is, the ignorance of darkness, and desire, and their **counterfeit spirit** — this is the cave for the remoulding of the body, which the robbers put upon the man: **the bond of forgetfulness**. And he became a mortal man. And this one who came down first, and the first separation...

**What is being said**

Someone who once gave something away and cannot take it back directly does the only thing left: sends help, hidden, into the very place the thing was taken to. Not a rescue that announces itself. A presence planted inside, doing the slow work alongside whatever is already suffering there, carrying it rather than lifting it out. And what that presence knows, and is there to teach, is one single fact: the road up is the same road that was walked coming down. There was never going to be a shortcut.

It has to stay hidden, because the ones who did the taking must not recognize what has been slipped in among their own work. And the sign that it is there at all is not the thing itself — nobody can point at it directly — but the shadow it throws. A shape can only be seen standing in its own light's shadow; that is simply what having an interior light does when the light itself cannot be looked at straight on.

And it works. The one carrying this hidden thing starts thinking past everyone who assembled him, and they see it happen, from above, and their answer is immediate and total: build another cage. Not out of nothing this time — out of everything at hand, earth, water, fire, every wind there is, stirred together into a single confusion, and pressed into the shape of death itself. And what is folded into that new shape, along with the earth and water and fire, is a spirit that only ever imitates — never the thing itself, always its resemblance — and forgetting, which is not a punishment handed down but a binding, tied on deliberately by hands that know exactly what they are doing and call themselves, without irony, the ones who came to help.

**Lexicon**

| Coptic | In the text | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| the Epinoia of light, called Life | the helper sent and hidden in Adam | assists, labors, "suffers with" — participation language, not rescue-from-outside language |
| "the way of ascent — the way by which he came down" | the Epinoia's teaching | there is no separate road; return retraces the descent |
| "the shadow of the light which is in him" | why the man becomes visible at all | shadow here is not absence but disclosure — the exterior trace an interior light throws |
| "the shadow of death" ... "the cave for the remoulding of the body" ... "the bond of forgetfulness" | the second body, built over the first | three names for one construction: a shape, a place, and what it does |
| "counterfeit spirit" | folded into the second body along with earth, water, fire | the same word used of Sophia's deviant offspring (Seam 8) and later of Fate (Seam 20) — kept identical in this translation throughout |

**Notes**

1. "Suffering with it" (fol. 20:19–20) is participation language, not rescue-from-a-distance. See AJ-029: the same verb recurs at fol. 28:1 of the Spirit, "who suffers with you," and compares to *Treatise on the Resurrection*'s "we suffered with him, and we arose with him." This tractate is a witness on the participation side of a wider passion-Christology question in the corpus, not the substitution side.
2. "The way of ascent — the way by which he came down" (fol. 20:23–24) is unbracketed. This reading follows it literally: there is no second route. Return is the descent, reversed.
3. "The shadow of the light which is in him" (fol. 20:28–30) is unbracketed. This reading treats shadow here as disclosure, not privation — the exterior sign of an interior a viewer cannot otherwise access.
4. This seam contains the tractate's clearest statement that mortality is an addition, not the human condition as such: the "bond of forgetfulness" and the "mortal man" are the outcome of a second act of moulding, performed on someone who was already alive and already shining. Compare AJ-032 (fol. 3:23–24, Seam 3): the refusal of the body/non-body split at the outset is consistent with the body here being treated as a garment added, not a fall into an evil substance.
5. Word for "counterfeit" is kept identical to its first use at fol. 10:4 (Sophia's deviant offspring, Seam 8) and its recurrence at fol. 28:13–16 (Fate, Seam 20) — see the header table.

## 16. The garden read part by part, and the tree
**fol. 21:15 – 22:20**

**The text in English**

> But the Epinoia of light which is in him is what will awaken his thinking. And the archons brought him and placed him in paradise. And they said to him: "Eat" — that is, at leisure. For indeed **their luxury is bitter and their beauty is lawless**. Their luxury is deception, and their trees are impiety, and their fruit is incurable poison, and their promise is death.
>
> But the tree of their life, which they placed in the middle of paradise — I shall teach you what is the mystery of their life, which is the plan they made together, **which is the likeness of their spirit**. This tree: its **root** is bitter, and its **branches** are death. Its **shadow** is hatred, and deception is in its **leaves**. And its **bloom** is the **anointing** of wickedness, and its **fruit** is death, and desire is its **seed**, and it blossoms from darkness. Those who taste of it — hades is their dwelling place, and darkness is their resting-place.
>
> But that which they call, among themselves, "the tree of knowing good and evil" — this is the **Epinoia of light**, which remains before him, so that he might not look up to his fullness and recognize the nakedness of his shamefulness. **But I caused them to eat.** And I said to the Savior: "Lord, was it not the serpent that taught Adam to eat?" And the Savior **laughed**, and said: "The serpent taught them to eat of wickedness, of a begetting of desire, of destruction, so that he would be useful to it. And it knew that Adam was disobedient to it because of the light of the Epinoia that is in him, which corrects his thought toward superiority over the first archon." And it wanted to bring out the power that it had put into him, through him. And it brought forgetfulness over Adam.

**What is being said**

They put him somewhere and tell him to enjoy himself, and the invitation is a lie built from the ground up: what they call luxury is bitter to the taste, what they call beauty has no order in it, what they call a tree is impiety grown tall, and the fruit at the end of the branch cannot be cured once eaten. Every word of welcome describes its own opposite.

And there is a tree in the middle of it that is not a decoration. It is the whole plan they made together, grown into wood, and it can be read the way anything grown can be read, one part at a time. The root, deep and out of sight, is bitterness — that is what feeds everything above it. The branches that reach out from the root are death, because that is what a bitter root can only produce further out. The shadow it casts over anyone standing under it is hatred. Its leaves, the part that looks soft and green from a distance, are deception up close. And its bloom — the part where something is anointed, made ready, consecrated — is wickedness receiving exactly that same anointing that was poured on the Autogenes at the very top of everything, except here it is poured on ruin instead. The fruit is death. The seed inside the fruit, waiting to grow another one just like it, is desire. Everyone who eats from it ends up living where it lives: in the dark, at rest in the one place rest costs nothing because nothing is asked of you there.

But there is another tree, and its name is a trick played on the reader more than on Adam. They call it the tree of knowing, as though knowing were the danger. What it actually is: the very presence that has been standing in front of him this whole time, close enough to touch, keeping him from looking up and seeing exactly how naked his makers' cleverness really is. Someone else, telling this story, says plainly: I made them eat it.

And the listener, hearing this, asks the obvious question — wasn't it the serpent who did the teaching? And the answer comes as laughter first, because the question has the right character and the wrong villain. The serpent did teach something, yes — it taught the archon's own hunger, desire feeding desire, aimed at making the man useful to it rather than free of it. And the serpent knew, even as it taught, that the man was already slipping out of its hand, because something was shining in him that the serpent had never put there and could not take credit for. So the one who lost that argument does what losers with power always do. Not defeat, exactly — an attempt to take back what was never his, followed by forgetting laid over the man like a lid.

**Notes**

1. The tree-reading (fol. 21:29–36) explicitly names itself: "the mystery of their life... the likeness of their spirit." This is the tractate's clearest self-declared instance of correspondential method — stating outright that a natural object is to be read part by part against an interior reality. See AJ-015.
2. "Its bloom is the anointing of wickedness" (fol. 21:33) uses the identical verb rendered "anointed" at fol. 6:23 of the Autogenes becoming Christ (Seam 6). 3. "I caused them to eat" (fol. 22:9) is a first-person claim by the speaker of the whole revelation (the Savior), stated flatly, without further defense at this point — the defense (the Isaiah citation) follows immediately in Seam 17.
4. John's question about the serpent, and the Savior's laughing correction, together form one continuous unit with the "not as Moses said" correction that opens Seam 17; the folio boundary used here (22:20/21) falls between two clauses of a single episode and is a convenience of this document's seam sizing, not a joint in the composition.

## 17. What could not be caught, and the veil lifted
**fol. 22:21 – 23:20**

**The text in English**

> And I said to the Savior: "What is forgetfulness?" And he said: "It is not as Moses wrote and you heard. For he said in his first book, 'He put him to sleep' — **but it was in his perception.** For he said through the prophet: 'I will make heavy their hearts, that they may not perceive, nor may not see.'" Then **the Epinoia of light hid herself in him**. And the chief archon wanted to bring her out of his rib. But **the Epinoia of light is ungraspable.** Although darkness pursued her, it did not catch her. And he brought a portion, out of his power, and made another creature, in the **shape** of a woman, according to the **likeness** of the Epinoia which had appeared to him. And he brought the portion which he had taken from the power of the man into the female creature, and not as Moses said, "his rib-bone."
>
> And he showed her to him in a form (**shape**) resembling herself, in that state; and Adam, seeing her, said: "You are bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh." For this reason man will leave his father and his mother and join to his wife, and the two will become one flesh, for his consort was sent to him. And she uncovered **the veil that was over his heart**. And he became sober from the **drunkenness** of darkness, and he **recognized his own likeness.**

**What is being said**

Someone asks what forgetting even is, and the answer refuses the word everyone already uses for it. Not sleep — a dulling. The heart made heavy on purpose, so that what could have been seen would not be seen and what could have been felt would not be felt. Sleep is what it looked like from outside. Numbness is what it was.

And the very thing they were trying to reach when they numbed him is exactly what slips away. He reaches into his own side for it and finds nothing there to hold, because what he is chasing has already hidden itself somewhere a hand cannot go. He chases anyway, the way darkness always chases what it cannot become, and it never catches up.

What he manages to build instead is not the thing itself but a resemblance of it, made from a piece of what he could actually get hold of — not the untouchable presence, only a portion of the man's own strength, shaped into a second figure standing next to the first. And when the man sees her, he does not see a stranger. He sees himself, finally recognizable in a shape that is not only his own. Something in him says: this is what I am, worn by someone else.

And then, at last, the thing that was hiding does what it was hidden there to do. It reaches up and pulls back a covering that had been laid over his heart the whole time — a covering he never knew was there because you cannot feel a veil from underneath it. And the moment it lifts, what had been a kind of drunkenness clears all at once, and he knows, plainly, without being told, what he actually is.

|---|---|---|

| "the Epinoia of light is ungraspable" | why the archon cannot extract her directly | the same word used of the Father's own light in Seam 4 ("the immeasurable light... ungraspable") — what cannot be seized above cannot be seized below either |

**Notes**

1. "Not as Moses said, 'his rib-bone'" (fol. 23:3–4) is the second of the tractate's three explicit corrections of the Genesis letter by this exact formula; the first was at fol. 13:19–21 (Seam 10, "above the waters"), the third occurs at fol. 29:6–8 (Seam 20, "in an ark"). See AJ-028. Each correction supplies what the composer takes to be the actual referent rather than discarding the passage.
2. The Isaiah 6:10 citation (fol. 22:25–28) is introduced as "the prophet," and is used to fix the sense of a different passage (Genesis 2:21) — one scriptural text glossing another. This is exegesis of shared scripture, not the invention of a rival mythology; see AJ-028 and the standing caution in this corpus against reading such vocabulary as foreign myth-making.
3. "The Epinoia of light is ungraspable" (fol. 22:30–32) uses the same root as "the immeasurable light... ungraspable" of the invisible Spirit at fol. 4:9–10 (Seam 4). This reading treats the recurrence as deliberate: what cannot be seized at the source cannot be seized in its hidden form either.
4. "The veil that was over his heart" (fol. 23:6) echoes 2 Corinthians 3:15 closely enough that this reading treats it as a scriptural allusion, not coincidence, consistent with this tractate's demonstrated habit of arguing from shared texts.
5. This seam is largely unbracketed and among the most secure textual ground in the tractate; no reading here rests on restoration.

## 18. The names put on the sons, and the water of forgetting
**fol. 23:21 – 25:15**

**The text in English**

> Now Sophia, who came down in innocence, so that she might restore her deficiency — for this reason she is called **Life**, that is, the mother of the living, by the forethought of the sovereignty of heaven. And through her they have tasted perfect knowledge. I appeared in the form of an eagle on the tree of knowledge, which is the Epinoia from the forethought of the pure light, so that I might teach them and awaken them from the depth of sleep. For they were both in a fallen state, and they recognized their nakedness. And the Epinoia appeared to them as light, awakening their thinking.
>
> And when Yaldabaoth recognized that they had withdrawn from him, he cursed his earth. He found the woman preparing herself for her husband. He was master over her, not knowing the mystery that had come to be through the holy counsel. And they were afraid to blame him. And he showed his angels his ignorance that is within him. And he cast them out of paradise, and clothed them in gloomy darkness. And the chief archon saw the virgin who stood beside Adam, and that the Epinoia of the light of life had appeared in her. And Yaldabaoth was filled with ignorance. And when the forethought of the All recognized it, she sent some, and they stole life away from Eve, and the chief archon defiled her, and begot in her two sons, the first and the second: **Eloim and Yave**. Eloim has a bear's face and Yave has a cat's face. One is just, the other unjust. Yave he set over fire and wind; Eloim he set over water and earth. **And these he called by the names Cain and Abel, with a view to his own cunning.**
>
> Until now, then, sexual intercourse has continued because of the chief archon; and he planted sexual desire in her who belongs to Adam. And he produced, through intercourse, the reproduction of the likeness of bodies, and he supplied them from his spirit — **the counterfeit**. And the two archons he set over the principalities, so that they might rule over the cave [of the body]. And when Adam recognized the likeness of his own foreknowledge, he begot the likeness of the son of man, and he called him **Seth**, according to the way of the generation in the aeons.
>
> Likewise the mother also sent down her spirit, which is in the likeness of herself, and a representation of those in the Pleroma, for she was to prepare a dwelling place for the aeons which would come down. **And she caused a water of forgetfulness to be given to him, through the chief archon, so that they would not know where they were from.** And thus the seed remained for a while, assisting, so that when the Spirit should come forth from the holy aeons, he would raise it up, and heal it of the deficiency, so that the whole Pleroma might become holy and without deficiency.

**What is being said**

Somewhere between the sleep and the shame, something that had once fallen begins climbing back, and the shape it takes to do it is a bird landing on a tree. Not to warn them off the tree — to tell them what it actually was, and to shake them awake from a sleep that had gone on so long it had started to feel like simply being alive. They wake, and the first thing waking gives them is not comfort. It is the sight of their own bareness, which they had never had to look at while asleep.

The one who thought he owned them notices the withdrawal before he understands it. He cannot name what has left his grip, only that something has, and his answer is to punish the ground itself, because the ground is the only thing still fully under him. He finds the woman getting ready, in a way he has no part in, and it does not occur to him that what he is looking at has already outgrown anything he arranged. He is master over her the way someone can be master over a house whose foundation has quietly shifted underneath him — the title still holds, the substance has already moved on. Ashamed of what he cannot see in himself, he throws them both out of the garden and dresses them in a darkness thick enough that even he can pretend it explains everything.

He watches the woman standing beside the man and sees something shining through her that he did not put there, and it fills him with exactly the same thing it has always filled him with. So he does what he has always done with a thing he cannot own outright: he takes it by force instead, and out of that comes two sons, one bear-faced, one cat-faced, one called just and one called unjust. And he gives them names everyone will remember forever — names taken straight out of the story his victims will go on to tell about themselves — chosen for exactly one reason: so the faces underneath will never be the ones anyone asks about.

Meanwhile the man recognizes, in himself, something that was always going to come from him regardless of what was done to the woman, and he names it after a foreknowledge that has nothing to do with the archon at all — a third son, whose name marks a line of descent that started somewhere else entirely and was only ever passing through this house.

And the last thing the mother does, before this stretch of the story closes, is arrange one more concealment — not a cloud this time but a drink, given so quietly that nobody notices they have been given anything at all. It does exactly what forgetting always does here: it keeps a seed intact and unransacked, hidden inside the not-knowing, for exactly as long as it takes for something to come and lift it back out again.

**Notes**

1. "He called them by the names Cain and Abel, with a view to his own cunning" (fol. 24:24–26) is unbracketed and is the tractate's second demonstration of the naming law stated at fol. 12:26–33. See AJ-025: the received scriptural names are stated by the text to be a cover, deliberately applied, and the applier is named. This reading treats Genesis's Cain and Abel as retained and reinterpreted, not discarded — consistent with this tractate's method throughout.
2. Two different accounts of Sophia's restoration now stand in this document: raised above her son at fol. 14:10–12 (Seam 11), and descending "in innocence" of her own accord here at fol. 23:21. See AJ-037. This reading records both without harmonizing them.
3. "The counterfeit" (of the spirit supplied through intercourse, fol. 24:31–32) is the third occurrence of this word in this translation, following Sophia's deviant offspring (Seam 8) and anticipating Fate (Seam 20).
4. Here it is inflicted deliberately, by the mother's own arrangement through the chief archon, as a temporary concealment rather than a permanent condition.
5. This reading treats it as a manuscript feature, not as doubled content, and does not draw any interpretive weight from the repetition itself.

## 19. The sorting of souls: what is saved, and what is not
**fol. 25:16 – 27:31**

**The text in English**

> And I said to him: "Lord, will all the souls, then, be saved into the pure light?" He answered and said to me: "Great things have arisen in your mind about this. It is difficult to explain them to any other than those who are from the unshakeable race.
>
> "Those on whom the Spirit of life will descend and join with the power will be saved, and will become perfect, and worthy of greatness, and will be purified in that place from all wickedness and the cares of malice, having no care for anything except incorruptibility alone, caring for it from now on, without anger or envy or jealousy or desire or insatiability of anything else. They are affected by nothing except the state of being in the flesh alone, which they wear, looking forward to the time when they will be met by those who receive them. Such are worthy of imperishable life and the calling; they endure everything and bear everything, so that they may finish the good, and inherit eternal life."
>
> And I said to him: "Lord, the souls of those who have not done these works, on whom the power of the Spirit of life has descended — what of them?" "**By all means they will be saved,** and be transferred. For the power descends upon **every** man, for without it none could stand upright. Once born, if the Spirit of life grows with them, the power comes to strengthen that soul, and no one can lead it astray to do the works of wickedness. But those upon whom the **counterfeit spirit** descends are drawn by it, and go astray."
>
> And I said: "Lord, the souls of these, when they come out of their flesh, where will they go?" And he **laughed**, and said to me: "The soul in which the power is greater than the despicable spirit is strong, and flees from evil through the intervention of the incorruptible one; it is saved, and taken up into the rest of the aeons."
>
> And I said: "Lord, what about those who did not know to whom they belong — where will their souls go?" And he said to me: "In those, the despicable spirit has grown strong when they go astray, and it weighs down the soul, drawing it into the works of wickedness, and casts it down into forgetfulness. And when it comes out, it is delivered to the authorities that came into being through the archon, and it is bound with chains and cast into prison, and goes around with it **until it awakes from forgetfulness and receives knowledge. And thus, when it is completed, it is saved.**"
>
> And I said: "Lord, how did the soul become smaller and enter again into the nature of its mother, or into man?" Then **he rejoiced** when I asked him this, and said to me: "Truly you are blessed, for you have understood. That soul will be made to follow another, in whom the Spirit of life is; thus she will be saved through him. **She will not be cast into another flesh again.**"
>
> And I said: "Lord, and those who have known, and turned away — where will their souls go?" Then he said to me: "To the place where the angels of poverty go, they will be taken there — the place where **there is no repentance**, and they will be kept for the day when those who have blasphemed against the Spirit will be tormented and punished with eternal punishment."

**What is being said**

He asks the largest question there is — does everyone get out? — and the answer starts by saying the question itself is a large one, too large to explain to just anyone. And then it sorts, patiently, one condition at a time.

Some carry the whole thing inside them already: the power joined all the way through, nothing left resisting it, no anger, no wanting, no envy, no hunger for anything but what they are already becoming. For them the flesh is the last thing left to endure, worn like a coat someone is about to take off, and they wear it out to the very end because that is what is asked of them, no more.

But then a harder question comes: what about the ones who received the same power and never did anything with it? And the answer refuses to be stingy. Saved regardless — every single one, because the power was never a reward for good behavior in the first place. It is what any of them stood on to be able to stand at all. Take it away and nobody, anywhere, remains upright.

Then a third case, further down: the ones under a spirit that only ever imitates, drawn wherever it pulls, weighed down, dropped into forgetting, handed over, chained, walked in circles for as long as it takes — until, and only until, the forgetting itself finally breaks. Then it is over. What is saved is saved once, and does not go back into flesh a second time to do it all again.

The one asking wants to know how anyone falls that far to begin with, and the asking itself pleases the one answering, more than any answer he has given so far — because the question shows the asker has understood something most people never even notice needs explaining.

And then, last, the one thing that does not get sorted into any of these categories at all: those who knew, plainly, and turned away anyway. Not the ignorant. Not the ones who never got anywhere. The ones who saw and chose otherwise. For them, no clock is running down toward release. There is simply no room built into this arrangement for coming back from that.

**Notes**

1. "By all means they will be saved... for the power descends upon every man" (fol. 26:10–14) is unbracketed and states, plainly, that salvation in the second category does not depend on works. This reading treats it as the tractate's considered position, not a concession.
2. "She will not be cast into another flesh again" (fol. 27:20–21) is unbracketed. This is the tractate's own statement on its terms: the remedial return described in the third category is terminating, not a standing metaphysical cycle. A categorical denial of any return to a body would go further than the text supports elsewhere (the third descent of the Pronoia goes *into* the body, Seam 21) — but a repeated, non-remedial cycle of embodiment is exactly what this sentence rules out for the case in question.
3. The Savior **laughs** here (fol. 26:26) and **rejoices** (fol. 27:15) — the only place in the tractate this second, warmer reaction occurs. Both responses come in answer to a question about mechanism (how souls move, how a soul could shrink back into a former nature) rather than a question about doctrine, and both are marked positively rather than as corrections of an error (contrast the laughter in Seams 10 and 16, which corrects a wrong reading).
4. "The place where there is no repentance" (fol. 27:26–27), for those who "have blasphemed against the Spirit," aligns with the synoptic saying on the unforgivable sin. This reading treats the alignment as evidence the composer is working with familiar Christian tradition, not inventing an unrelated category.
5. This catechism runs as one sustained dialogue (four rounds of question and answer); it is presented above in close paraphrase rather than word-for-word quotation throughout, to keep the length proportionate. No restoration bears significant weight in this seam; the dialogue is largely unbracketed.

## 20. Fate, the cloud, and the counterfeit that grows old without truth
**fol. 28:1 – 30:4**

**The text in English**

> "...where did the counterfeit spirit come from?" Then he said to me: "The Mother-Father, of great mercy, the holy Spirit in every form, the compassionate one, **who suffers with you** — that is, the Epinoia of the Forethought of light — awakened the seed of the generation of the perfect, and their thought, and the eternal light of man. And when the chief archon recognized that they surpassed him in loftiness of thought, and thought more clearly than he, he wanted to seize their thought, being ignorant that they surpassed him in thought, and that he could not seize them. He took counsel with his authorities, which are his powers, and together they **committed adultery with Sophia**, and bitter Fate (**Heimarmene**) was begotten through them — **the last of the changeable, counterfeit bonds**. And it is of a kind counterfeit to one another, hard, and strong — this Fate which the gods and angels and demons and all generations have obeyed, until now. For from that fate came forth every sin and injustice and blasphemy, and the bond of forgetfulness, and ignorance, and every burdensome commandment and grave sins and great fears. And thus the whole creation was made blind, so that they might not know the God who is above them all. And because of the bond of forgetfulness their sins have been hidden. **For they are bound with measures and times and seasons** — Fate being lord over all of it."
>
> And he [the chief archon] took thought for everything that came to be through him. And again he made a plan to bring a flood upon the creation of man. But the majesty of the light of Forethought instructed Noah, and he preached to the whole seed which is the sons of men, and those who were strangers to him did not listen to him — **not as Moses said, that they hid themselves in an ark** — but they hid themselves in a place, not only Noah, but also many other people from the unshakeable race. They went into a place, and hid themselves in **a luminous cloud**. And he recognized his sovereignty, and the light that had enlightened him was with him — for he [the archon] had brought darkness on the whole earth.
>
> ... They gathered together again with one another and made a plan; they created a **despicable spirit** in the likeness of the Spirit that had come down, so that through it they might defile the souls. And **they altered their likeness** — the angels — to resemble the women's own partners, filling them with the spirit of darkness which they had mixed for them, and wickedness. They brought gold and silver and a gift and copper and iron and metal and all kinds of things, and they drew men into great cares which they had brought upon them, leading them astray in much error. **They grew old without enjoyment, and died having found no truth, and without knowing the God of truth.**

**What is being said**

Someone asks where the imitation itself first came from, and the answer starts, unexpectedly, with mercy — the same mercy that has been sending help this whole time, waking a seed that was always going to wake. And that is exactly what the one still ruling below cannot stand to watch happen without him. He cannot take hold of what has outgrown him, so he does what every failed seizure eventually does: it turns political. He gathers his own authorities and forces himself, together with them, on the very source of everything he has ever had — and out of that violation something is born that nobody asked for and everybody afterward has to live inside: a binding, dressed up as law, made of measured hours and appointed seasons, calling itself the shape the world simply has to take.

It works on everyone. Not because it is strong in the way truth is strong, but because forgetting is built into it from the beginning, and forgetting is very good at making an arrangement look inevitable rather than manufactured. Every failure anyone commits afterward gets absorbed into it and hidden by it at the same time — as though a schedule could also be a confession, kept sealed.

And the one who set all this in motion is not finished. Watching what he made outgrow him once already, he decides to erase the experiment and start again, and reaches for water this time, the whole sky's worth of it. But the light that has been teaching from the very beginning gets there first, and what it teaches is not a boat. It teaches a hiding place — bright enough to be a shelter and thick enough to be invisible at the same time — and more people than the one name everyone remembers walk into it and are not found. The flood arrives, does everything it was sent to do, and still does not reach what it was sent for.

And having failed at drowning them, the last resort is imitation, closer and more intimate than any before it: copies of the very thing that had been sent down as protection, worn like a disguise, changing the very faces the women already trust, arriving as husbands, as lovers, as familiar shapes, carrying gifts made of exactly the things people cannot stop wanting — metal, ornament, wealth with no bottom to it — until an entire population is worn down not by force but by being kept endlessly, comfortably busy. They live their whole lives without ever once being satisfied, and they die the same way, never having touched anything true, never once having known who it was that made them.

Heimarmene (Fate) is not treated as the underlying structure of the cosmos but as a *product*, begotten through an act of violation, and named with the same word ("counterfeit") used of Sophia's deviant offspring (Seam 8) and the second body's spirit (Seam 15). See AJ-038: this is shared Hellenistic vocabulary for astral determinism, but the tractate's specific move — treating Fate as manufactured rather than given — is its own.
2. "Not as Moses said, that they hid themselves in an ark" (fol. 29:6–8) is the third and last of the tractate's explicit corrections of the Genesis letter by this formula (see Seams 10 and 17 for the first two). See AJ-028.
3. AJ-039 records the luminous cloud as a single correspondence used in opposite senses across the tractate: concealment turned against the light when Sophia hides her unfinished offspring (fol. 10:14–19, Seam 8), concealment used to preserve the light when the unshakeable race hides from the flood (fol. 29:11–12, here). This reading treats the recurrence as deliberate, not incidental.
4. "They altered their likeness" (fol. 29:26) is the tractate's clearest statement of counterfeit polymorphy, and this reading places it in direct contrast with Seam 2's theophany: one form under three shapes, appearing through one another, is revelation; altering the likeness itself to deceive is its exact opposite. Both are rendered "changed his appearance" in ordinary English translation, which erases the distinction the Coptic maintains throughout.
5. "They grew old without enjoyment, and died having found no truth, and without knowing the God of truth" (fol. 30:2–4) closes this seam on the same diagnosis that opened the tractate and recurs throughout: not knowing the source.

## 21. The three descents, the seal, and the charge to secrecy
**fol. 30:5 – 32:9**

**The text in English**

> ... the whole creation became enslaved forever, from the foundation of the world until now. And they took women and begot children in darkness, according to the likeness of their spirit, and closed their hearts, and hardened them by the hardness of the **counterfeit spirit**, until now.
>
> **I, then, the perfect Forethought of the All, transformed myself into my seed** — for I existed from the first, walking in every road. For I am the richness of the light; I am **the remembrance of the Pleroma**.
>
> I walked in the greatness of the darkness, and I persevered until I entered into the midst of the prison. And the foundations of chaos shook. And **I hid myself from them because of their wickedness, and they did not know me.**
>
> Again I returned a second time, and I walked, I came forth from those who belong to my light. I entered into the midst of darkness and the inside of Hades, seeking after my administration. And the foundations of chaos shook, **lest they fall upon those who are in the chaos and be destroyed before the time.** And again I hastened back to the root of the light, that they might not be destroyed before the time.
>
> Yet **a third time** I went — I who am the light existing in the light. I am the remembrance of the Forethought, that I might enter into the midst of darkness, and the inside of Hades. And I filled my face with the light of the completion of their aeon. And I entered into the midst of their **prison — this is the prison of the body.** And I said: **"He who hears, arise from the heavy sleep."** And he wept, and shed heavy tears. He wiped them away and said: **"Who is it that calls my name, and from where has this hope come to me while I am in the chains of the prison?"** And I said: "I am the Forethought of the pure light. I am the thought of the virginal Spirit, who raises you up to the honored place. **Arise, and remember** that you are the one who has heard, and follow **your root, which is I, the merciful one.** And guard yourself against the angels of poverty, and the demons of chaos, and all who ensnare you. And be watchful of the heavy sleep and of the enclosure of the inside of Hades."
>
> And I raised him up, and **I sealed him in the light of the water with five seals, that death might not have power over him from this time on.** And behold, now I go up to the perfect aeon. I have completed everything for you in your hearing. And I have told you everything, that you may write them down and give them, **in secret, to your fellow spirits — for this is the mystery of the unshakeable race.** And the Savior gave this to me, so that I might write it down and keep it securely. And he said to him: **"Cursed be everyone who will exchange these things for a gift, or for food, or drink, or clothing, or any other such thing."**
>
> And these things were given to him in a mystery. And immediately he vanished from before him, and he went to his fellow disciples, and told them what the Savior had said to him.
>
> Jesus Christ. Amen.
>
> According to John. The Apocryphon.

**What is being said**

I have always been here. I am not arriving for the first time; I am changing into a shape small enough to fit through the door that has been shut this whole time. What I am is memory — not a record kept somewhere else, but the thing itself remembering the whole, and being that remembering.

The first time I go in, I go all the way into the greatest dark there is, and I stay hidden the entire time, because they cannot be trusted with what I am and I do not offer it. The ground under everyone there shakes just from my passing through, and still nobody there ever learns I was there at all.

The second time I go further, looking now for something specific rather than only enduring — my own administration, the order that belongs to me and has been misplaced. And the ground shakes again, worse this time, enough that I have to leave before it breaks entirely, because if it breaks too soon everyone caught in it breaks with it, and that is not yet the time for that.

The third time is the one that finally reaches all the way down, past the dark and past the underworld and into the innermost cell — the body itself, which turns out to have been the prison the whole time, not a separate place from the darkness but its final, most enclosed room. And there I say the one thing that matters: wake up.

And he does. Not calmly — he weeps first, heavy tears, the kind that come from someone realizing how long they have actually been asleep. And the first thing out of his mouth is not gratitude. It is a question: who is calling me, and where did this hope even come from, here, chained, in the dark? And the answer he gets back is not new information. It is what he already was, telling him what he already was: you are the one who heard. Follow your root. I am that root, and I am the merciful one, and I have always been exactly that, whether you were awake to it or not.

I lift him, and I close the seal over him in the same light the very first water ever held, so that nothing that took him down before can take him down again. And then I say the only thing left to say: I have finished. Everything you needed has now passed through your own hearing. Write it, and give it to the others who are made of what you are made of — quietly, without display, and never, under any condition, for a price. Whoever trades this for anything — food, drink, clothing, a gift, anything at all — is cursed for the trade, not for the telling. Telling was always the point. Selling is the one thing that turns this into exactly the kind of theft the whole story has been about.

And then he is simply gone, and the one who received it goes and tells the others, and that is the whole of it.

**Notes**

1. "The prison of the body" (fol. 31:3–4) is unbracketed and is the tractate's own gloss on what "their prison" means at the climax of the three descents. This confirms, at the structural climax of the whole work, the position argued from fol. 3:23–24 (Seam 3, AJ-032): the body is not what the light is delivered *from*; it is where the light finally arrives.
2. The three descents are commonly read against the Manichaean pattern of a light-emissary sent down deliberately as bait. This tractate's Pronoia descends on her own account, in continuity with Sophia's own initiative at fol. 9:26 (Seam 8) rather than as a commissioned mission; this is a real point of divergence from that witness, not a variant of the same story, and this reading does not smooth it over.
3. "I sealed him in the light of the water with five seals" (fol. 31:23–25) closes the water-image that opened at fol. 4:20–26 (Seam 5, the Father's spring) and recurred at fol. 14:33–15:8 (Seam 12, the archons' reflection). See AJ-026: one image, three occurrences, and the third completes rather than merely repeats the first.
4. The closing curse (fol. 31:34–37) is on exchange, not on speech: cursed is whoever trades the teaching for payment in any form, not whoever tells it. This reading treats the distinction as load-bearing, not incidental — it is the same failure, at personal scale, as the chief archon's throughout the tractate: receiving something and treating it as one's own stock to spend.
5. The colophon ("According to John. The Apocryphon.") is the scribal title, given at the end rather than the beginning, as is conventional for this genre of manuscript.

## Where this reading goes beyond the text

Named, so the reading can be checked. This list concerns the *interpretation* laid over the established text, not the reliability of the text itself.

1. **The frame echo between fol. 1:11–12 and fol. 11:21–22** ("the place he came from"). The lexical parallel stands at both ends. That the composer *intended* it as a deliberate inclusio framing the whole tractate — John possessing the knowledge the archon lacks — is this reading's inference, built on that parallel, not a claim the text makes about itself.
2. **Reading John's departure from the temple as a state rather than a route** (Seam 1). The text gives the movement — away, to a deserted place, grieving; the "empty ground" reading of what that departure *means* is interpretive.
3. **The "one form, three shapes" principle (Seam 2) extended as a reading-key across the whole tractate.** The claim is solid where the text sets up an explicit doublet (Seams 2, 8, 20). Treating "constant interior / variable exterior" as the tractate's single governing principle, and using it to organize the whole of this document's approach to translation, is a reading built on real but scattered evidence, not a claim the tractate states as a unified rule in one place.
4. **The apophatic Monad (Seam 3) as "declining a frame the rest of the book will need."** The double negation of corporeal/incorporeal is manuscript fact. That the composer placed it there *deliberately to forestall* a later misreading of the body is this reading's inference about authorial intention.
5. **"Will, then word, then work" (Seam 7) read as the tractate's stated premise, later inverted by Sophia (Seam 8).** Both halves of the parallel are manuscript fact. That the composer intended the second as a deliberate echo of the first, rather than the two being independent formulas that happen to share vocabulary, is this reading's inference from the shared terms.
6. **Sophia (Seams 8, 11, 18) receiving no moral verdict.** The text does not condemn her; it states missing elements and results. Extending that restraint into a positive claim that the tractate treats her fault as *structural rather than moral in kind* is this reading's extrapolation from an absence.
7. **The image-in-water sequence (Seams 5, 12, 21) as a single deliberate circuit.** The three occurrences and the recurring verb are manuscript fact. Treating them as one authorial construction, rather than three uses of common imagery that happen to recur, is this reading's synthesis.
8. **"Envy makes the skin, and envy is what the powers feel when the man shines" (Seams 13–14) as one deliberate construction.** Both halves use the same root. That the composer meant the second to be read against the first is this reading's inference from the shared word, not a stated cross-reference in the text.
9. **Shadow as disclosure rather than privation (Seam 15).** The text says the man became visible "because of the shadow of the light... in him." That shadow specifically signifies *revelation of an interior* rather than simply describing a visual effect of a lit object is this reading's philosophical gloss on a single clause.
10. **The tree read "part by part" (Seam 16) generalized as a demonstration of method.** The text does perform this operation on the tree explicitly and calls it "the likeness of their spirit." Treating this single passage as proof that the whole body-catalogue (Seam 13) should be read the same way is an inference this reading draws but the text does not state.
11. **"Nothing is corrected at a degree above where it landed"** (used to explain why the third descent must reach the body, Seam 21) is not a sentence in the tractate. It is this reading's formulation of why the narrative requires three descents rather than one, offered as an interpretive principle, not a quotation.
12. **The curse on selling (Seam 21) read as the chief archon's error repeated at personal scale.** The curse itself, and its target (exchange, not speech), are manuscript fact. The parallel to the archon's pattern of receiving-and-claiming is this reading's connection, not a comparison the text draws itself.
13. **Six anomalies are recorded rather than resolved**, per the standing rule against harmonizing what the Coptic does not harmonize: the mixed register of the Pentad (Seam 6, Notes 4); the flesh/`ⲥⲁⲣⲝ` exception among the substance-souls (Seam 13, Notes 1); the two differing accounts of Sophia's restoration — raised above her son (Seam 11) versus descending in innocence (Seam 18); the twelve-named-authorities list standing unreconciled beside the seven animal-faced powers (Seam 9, Notes 1); the shared Hellenistic inventory of elements, passions, and Fate, whose presence marks the composer's world without by itself proving a distinctive transmission (Seams 13, 20); and the divergence from the Manichaean commissioned-descent pattern (Seam 21, Notes 2).
14. **The four elemental rulers as a Swedenborgian compass of cardinal directions**, sometimes argued elsewhere in this corpus's findings from English-derived name etymologies, is **not** asserted anywhere in this reading. Seam 13 records the four qualities and their common mother as shared Hellenistic physics and explicitly declines to build the stronger claim from them; see AJ-038.
