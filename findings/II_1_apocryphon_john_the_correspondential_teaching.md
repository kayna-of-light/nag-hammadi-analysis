## 0. What this document is, and what it rests on

This is a reading of the **Coptic** of the long recension (NHC II,1), folio by folio. The English translation is not the object here. That is not a preference; it is a necessity. The load-bearing evidence in this tractate is **lexical register** — which words are native Egyptian and which are Greek loans, and where the text switches between them. An English translation renders `ⲅⲛⲱⲥⲓⲥ` and `ⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ` both as "knowledge", `ⲭⲁⲣⲓⲥ` and `ϩⲙⲟⲧ` both as "grace", `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ` and `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ` and `ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` and `ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ` all as "form" or "likeness". Every one of those collapses destroys a distinction the Coptic is making deliberately and consistently.

**Constraints on every quotation below.** Linssen's conventions are retained: `[...]` = editorial reconstruction of a lacuna, `{{...}}` = scribal deletion, `˙` and `'` = punctuation/stroke marks, combining macron = supralinear stroke. Codex II is heavily damaged in places. Where a quotation carries brackets, the bracketed portion is **restoration, not manuscript**, and no argument here rests on a bracketed word alone. Every claim below that carries weight is anchored in unbracketed text.

**Labelling.** Each finding is marked **[OBSERVATION]** (the text says this; anyone reading the Coptic will find it) or **[READING]** (this is my inference from what the text says, and could be argued against).

## 1. The governing distinction: `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ` / `ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` against `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ` / `ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ`

The tractate opens with the revealer appearing to John in changing shapes. The Coptic does not say he changed. It says something much more precise.

> `ⲟⲩⲉⲓⲛⲉ ⲉϥⲟ ⲛ̅ϩⲁϩ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ` — *a likeness (`ⲉⲓⲛⲉ`) having many forms (`ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`)* — fol. 2:6
>
> `ⲡⲉⲥⲙⲁⲧ ⲛⲁϥⲟ ⲛ̅ϣⲟⲙⲧⲉ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ` — *the `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ` was in three `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲁⲓ`* — fol. 2:8–9

One native-named constant (`ⲉⲓⲛⲉ`, `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ`); three Greek-named appearances (`ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`) — child, old man, servant. **[OBSERVATION]**

This is not a stylistic accident. The pair holds across all thirty-two folios and does real work at every crux:

| Folio | Native `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ`/`ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` | Greek `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`/`ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ` | What the pairing marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2:6–9 | the revealer's one `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ` | three `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲁⲓ` | constant state, variable form |
| 10:4–9 | `ⲉϥϣⲃ̅ⲃⲓⲁⲉⲓⲧ ⲁⲡⲉⲥⲥⲙⲟⲧ` (different from her `ⲥⲙⲟⲧ`); `ⲟⲩⲁⲧⲥⲙⲟⲧ` (without `ⲥⲙⲟⲧ`) relative to `ⲡⲉⲓⲛⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉϥⲙⲁⲁⲩ` | `ⲁϥⲟ ⲛ̅ϭⲉⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`; `ⲟⲩⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ ⲉϥϣⲃⲃⲓⲁⲉⲓⲧ` | the malformation: deviation *from* the interior likeness |
| 14:34 / 15:8 | — | `ⲁⲡⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ ⲛ̅ⲧϩⲓⲕⲱⲛ` (the `ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ` of the image) | the archons see only an imprint of an image |
| 22:35–23:2 | `ⲕⲁⲧⲁ ⲡⲓⲛⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉⲡⲓⲛⲟⲓⲁ` | `ϩⲛ̅ ⲟⲩⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ ⲛ̅ⲥϩⲓⲙⲉ` | an outward female shape cast after an interior likeness |
| 23:27 | `ⲙ̅ⲡⲥⲙⲁⲧ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲁⲉⲧⲟⲥ` (in the `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ` of an eagle) | — | the same one `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ`, another appearance |
| 27:35 | `ϩⲛ̅ ⲥⲙⲁⲧ ⲛⲓⲙ` — the Spirit *in every* `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ` | — | the principle stated as a property of the Spirit |
| 29:26 | `ⲁⲩϣⲃ̅ⲧⲟⲩϩⲙ̅ ⲡⲟⲩⲉⲓⲛⲉ` — the angels **altered their `ⲉⲓⲛⲉ`** | — | the counterfeit of polymorphy |

**[READING]** The pattern is that `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ`/`ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` names what a thing *is* at the interior degree, and `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`/`ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ` names how it *presents* at the exterior. The revealer holds one `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ` beneath three `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲁⲓ`; the counterfeiting angels at fol. 29:26 do the opposite — they change the `ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` itself. The text therefore draws a boundary the English cannot show: **variable form over a constant state is revelation; altering the state to fit a form is deception.** They are not the same operation and the Coptic does not use the same words for them.

## 2. The refusal of the body / non-body split

The apophatic description of the Monad runs a long chain of native `ⲁⲧ-` privatives: `ⲟⲩⲁⲧϣⲓⲧϥ` (immeasurable), `ⲟⲩⲁⲧϯⲣⲁⲛ ⲉⲣⲟϥ` (unnameable), `ⲟⲩⲁⲧϣⲁϫⲉ ⲉⲣⲟϥ` (ineffable), `ⲟⲩⲁⲧⲛⲁⲩ ⲉⲣⲟϥ` (invisible). Then, at fol. 3:23–24:

> `ⲟⲩⲥⲱⲙⲁⲧⲓⲕⲟⲥ ⲁⲛ ⲡⲉ ⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲟⲩⲁⲧⲥⲱⲙⲁ ⲁⲛ ⲡⲉ` — *he is not corporeal, nor is he incorporeal*

**[OBSERVATION]** The text explicitly declines both halves of the body/non-body dichotomy.

**[READING]** This is the tractate refusing at the outset the frame that a later interpreter would need in order to read the whole cosmogony as a flight *from* matter. The negation is symmetrical. Whatever the descent into the body means in this text, it does not mean that the real is defined as the not-material.

## 3. Influx written into the lexicon

This is the strongest single finding in the tractate, and it is invisible in translation. Folio 4:1–9 runs a series of predications of the form *he is X, giving x*. The register is not random:

> `ⲟⲩⲅⲛⲱⲥⲓⲥ ⲡⲉ ⲉϥϯ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ` — *he is `ⲅⲛⲱⲥⲓⲥ`, giving `ⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ`*
>
> `ⲟⲩⲭⲁⲣⲓⲥ ⲡⲉ ⲉϥϯ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩϩⲙⲟⲧ` — *he is `ⲭⲁⲣⲓⲥ`, giving `ϩⲙⲟⲧ`*
>
> `ⲟⲩⲛⲁⲉ ⲡⲉ ⲉϥϯ ⲛ̅ⲛⲟⲩⲛⲁ ⲙⲛ̅ ⲟⲩⲥⲱⲧⲉ` — *he is mercy, giving mercy and redemption*
>
> `ⲟⲩⲁⲅⲁⲑⲟⲥ ⲡⲉ ⲉϥϯ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧ̅ⲁⲅⲁⲑⲟⲥ` … `ⲟⲩⲁⲓⲱⲛ ⲡⲉ ⲉϥϯ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲁⲓⲱⲛ` … `ⲟⲩⲱⲛϩ ⲡⲉ ⲉϥϯ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲱⲛϩ`

**[OBSERVATION]** In the two cases where the language offers a genuine doublet, the Greek loan names what he **is** and the native Egyptian word names what he **gives**: `ⲅⲛⲱⲥⲓⲥ` → `ⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ`, `ⲭⲁⲣⲓⲥ` → `ϩⲙⲟⲧ`. These are the same word twice over in any translation.

**[READING]** The construction says one reality is being named at two degrees, and the shift of degree is carried by the shift of register. This is the same signature as the εἰκών → `ⲙⲉ` pairing in Silvanus (VII,4 fol. 100:27–31), where a Greek loan names the outer image and native Egyptian words name what it discloses. Two independent tractates, the same device. Neither is visible in English.

## 4. Will, then word, then work — stated as a sequence

> `ⲡⲉϥⲟⲩⲱϣ ⲁϥϣⲱⲡⲉ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲉⲣⲅⲟⲛ` — *his **will** (`ⲟⲩⲱϣ`) became a **work** (`ⲉⲣⲅⲟⲛ`)* — fol. 7:6–7
>
> `ⲡϣⲁϫⲉ ⲁϥⲟⲩⲁϩϥ ⲛ̅ⲥⲁ ⲡⲟⲩⲱϣⲉ` — *the **word** (`ϣⲁϫⲉ`) followed the will* — fol. 7:8–9
>
> `ⲉⲧⲃⲉ ⲡϣⲁϫⲉ ⲅⲁⲣ ⲁϥⲧⲁⲙⲓⲟ ⲙ̅ⲡⲧⲏⲣϥ ⲛ̅ϭⲓ ⲡⲉⲭ̅ⲥ̅ ⲡⲁⲩⲧⲟⲅⲉⲛⲏⲥ` — *for through the word Christ the Autogenes created the all* — fol. 7:10–11

**[OBSERVATION]** The order is given explicitly and the dependency is stated in words: the word *followed* the will. Not simultaneous, not interchangeable.

This is the pivot of the entire tractate, because the fall is narrated as its exact inversion (§7).

## 5. The emanation liturgy: a five-step formula for reception

For each aeon of the Pentad, folios 5–6 run the same sequence:

1. she **asks** — `ⲁⲓⲧⲉⲓ`
2. he **gazes** — `ⲁϥⲉⲓⲱⲣⲙ`
3. it **is revealed** — `ⲁⲥϭⲱⲗⲡ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ`
4. it **stands** — `ⲁⲥⲁϩⲉⲣⲁⲧⲥ̅`
5. it **gives glory** — `ⲁⲥϯ ⲉⲟⲟⲩ`

**[OBSERVATION]** Nothing comes into being without being asked for; the giving is a *look*; and the circuit closes with glory returned to the source.

**[OBSERVATION — the same verb, the opposite result]** `ⲉⲓⲱⲣⲙ` recurs at fol. 14:30 and 20:32 for the archons' gaze — `ⲛ̅ⲧⲁⲣⲟⲩⲉⲓⲱⲣⲙⲉ ⲛ̅ϭⲓ ⲛⲉⲝⲟⲩⲥⲓⲁ ⲧⲏⲣⲟⲩ` — and what they see is a reflection they then copy. One verb; generation above, imitation below. The difference is not in the act but in the state of the one acting.

## 6. The Divine Human at the top

Barbelo, produced by the Father's gaze into the spring, is titled:

- `ⲁⲥϣⲱⲡⲉ ⲙ̅ⲙⲏⲧⲣⲁ ⲙ̅ⲡⲧⲏⲣϥ` — the womb of the all (fol. 5:5)
- `ⲧⲙⲏⲧⲣⲟⲡⲁⲧⲱⲣ` — Mother-Father (fol. 5:7)
- **`ⲡϣⲟⲣⲡ ⲛ̅ⲣⲱⲙⲉ`** — **the first man** (fol. 5:7)
- `ⲑⲓⲕⲱⲛ ⲙ̅ⲡⲓⲁϩⲟⲣⲁⲧⲟⲥ ⲙ̅ⲡⲛ̅ⲁ̅` — the image of the invisible Spirit
- `ϩⲟⲟⲩⲧ ⲥϩⲓⲙⲉ` — male-female

And later, from the height, the voice: `ϥϣⲟⲟⲡ' ⲛ̅ϭⲓ ⲡⲣⲱⲙⲉ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲡϣⲏⲣⲉ ⲙ̅ⲡⲣⲱⲙⲉ` — *Man exists, and the Son of Man* (fol. 14:14–15), followed by `ϩⲛ̅ ⲟⲩⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ ⲛ̅ⲁⲛⲇⲣⲉⲁⲥ ⲁϥⲟⲩⲱⲛϩ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲙ̅ⲡⲉϥⲉⲓⲛⲉ` — *in a human form he revealed his likeness* (fol. 14:23–24).

**[OBSERVATION]** The human form is not the endpoint of the descent in this text. It is the form of the *first* emanation and of the Father's self-disclosure. The anthropos is at the top.

## 7. Sophia: thought without will — the inversion of §4

> `ⲁⲥⲙⲉⲉⲩⲉ ϩⲛ̅ ⲟⲩⲙⲉⲉⲩⲉ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲛ̅ϩⲏⲧⲥ̅` — *she thought a thought **out of herself*** — fol. 9:26
>
> `ⲁϫⲙ̣̅ [ⲡⲟⲩⲱ]ϣ ⲙ̅ⲡⲉⲡ̅ⲛ̅ⲁ̅` — without the **will** of the Spirit — fol. 9:29
>
> `ⲁⲩ[ⲱ ⲁϫⲙ̅ ⲡ]ⲉ̣ⲥϣⲃⲣ̅ ⲛ̅ϩⲱⲧⲣ̅` — and without her **consort** — fol. 9:30
>
> `ⲁⲥⲙⲟⲕⲙⲉⲕ ⲇⲉ ⲭⲱⲣⲓⲥ ⲡⲟⲩⲱϣ ⲙ̅ⲡⲉⲡ̅ⲛ̅ⲁ̅ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲡⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ ⲙ̅ⲡⲉⲥϫⲱⲛϥ` — *she deliberated without the will of the Spirit and the knowledge of her partner* — fol. 9:34–35

**[OBSERVATION]** The fault is named three times over in one paragraph, and each time the missing element is **`ⲟⲩⲱϣ`** — will — or the consort who supplies it. `ϣⲃⲣ̅ ⲛ̅ϩⲱⲧⲣ̅` is a native compound: literally *yoke-fellow*, from `ϩⲱⲧⲣ`, to join or yoke.

**[READING]** Two folios earlier the text gave the order: will, then word, then work. Here the same vocabulary is used to narrate what goes wrong: thought proceeding without the will it should follow. This is not a general statement about pride or ignorance. It is the specific inversion of the sequence the text has just established, expressed in the same words. Wisdom acting apart from love, named as such, in the tractate's own terms.

Note also what the text does **not** say: it does not condemn her. Her act produces something malformed, and the whole remainder of the cosmogony is the correction of it, with her repentance (fol. 13:23, 13:36–14:12) at the centre of the repair.

## 8. The malformation named in the same vocabulary

> `ⲟⲩϩⲱⲃ ⲛ̅ⲁⲧϫⲱⲕ` — an imperfect thing
> `ⲉϥϣⲃ̅ⲃⲓⲁⲉⲓⲧ ⲁⲡⲉⲥⲥⲙⲟⲧ` — different from her `ⲥⲙⲟⲧ`
> `ⲛⲉ ⲟⲩⲁⲧⲥⲙⲟⲧ ⲡⲉ ⲁⲡⲉⲓⲛⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉϥⲙⲁⲁⲩ` — it was without `ⲥⲙⲟⲧ` relative to the `ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` of his mother
> `ⲁϥⲟ ⲛ̅ϭⲉⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ` — he was of another `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`
> `ⲟⲩⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ ⲉϥϣⲃⲃⲓⲁⲉⲓⲧ ⲛ̅ⲇⲣⲁⲕⲱⲛ ⲛ̅ϩⲟ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲩⲉⲓ` — a different `ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ`: a serpent with a **lion's face**
> — fol. 10:3–9

**[OBSERVATION]** The deviation is expressed exactly in the `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ`/`ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` vs `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`/`ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ` vocabulary of the opening theophany, now in the negative sense. What was there a constant beneath many appearances is here an appearance with no constant behind it.

**[READING]** The lion's face is worth flagging against Thomas 7, where the lion is the power of self-love devouring, and against the general assignment of animals to affections. Here it is compounded: serpent (the sensory faculty) wearing the face of the lion (the appetite that consumes). The composite is the sensory mind under the rule of the appetite — which is precisely what the figure goes on to do.

## 9. Mixture as sickness — the tractate's account of what evil is

> `ⲛ̅ⲧⲟϥ ⲅⲁⲣ ⲟⲩⲕⲁⲕⲉ ⲛ̅ⲁⲧⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛⲉ ⲡⲉ` — *for he is an ignorant darkness*
>
> `ⲡⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ⲇⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲁⲣⲉϥⲧⲱϩ ⲙⲛ̅ ⲡⲕⲁⲕⲉ ⲁϥⲧⲣⲉⲡⲕⲁⲕⲉ ⲣ̅ ⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ⲡⲕⲁⲕⲉ ⲇⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲁⲣⲉϥⲧⲱϩ ⲙⲛ̅ ⲡⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ⲁϥϩⲧⲙ̅ⲧⲙ̅ ⲡⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲙ̅ⲡϥϣⲱⲡⲉ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲛ̅ⲕⲁⲕⲉ ⲁⲗⲗⲁ ⲁϥϣⲱⲡ̣ⲉ ⲉϥϣⲟⲛⲉ`
>
> *when the light mixed with the darkness it made the darkness shine; when the darkness mixed with the light it dimmed the light, and it became **neither light nor darkness but became sick** (`ⲉϥϣⲟⲛⲉ`)* — fol. 11:10–15

And immediately: `ⲡⲓⲁⲣⲭⲱⲛ ϭⲉ ⲉⲧϣⲟⲛⲉ` — *this archon, then, who is sick*.

**[OBSERVATION]** The word is `ϣⲱⲛⲉ`, the ordinary native verb for being ill. The first ruler is not defined as a substance opposed to the good. He is defined as a **disorder** — an improper mixing that produced neither of its terms.

**[READING]** This matters for how the tractate's dualism should be classified. The two principles here are not two substances. Light and darkness mix, and what results is a pathology, not a third substance. Evil in this text has the ontological status of a disease.

## 10. The boast, and the diagnosis given in the same breath

> `ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲛⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲙⲛ̅ ⲕⲉⲛⲟⲩⲧⲉ ϣⲟⲟⲡ ⲛ̅ⲥⲁⲃⲗ̅ⲗⲏⲉⲓ` — *I am God and there is no other God beside me*
>
> `ⲉϥⲟ ⲛ̅ⲁⲧⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ ⲙ̅ⲡⲉϥⲧⲁϫⲣⲟ ⲡⲙⲁ ⲉⲛⲧⲁϥⲉⲓ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲙ̅ⲙⲁⲩ` — *being ignorant of his own strength, **the place from which he came***
> — fol. 11:19–22

**[OBSERVATION]** The text supplies the diagnosis in the same sentence as the claim, and the diagnosis is about **source**. Not "he is wicked" — *he does not know where his power came from.*

The second form of the boast is Exodus 20:5 verbatim, with the text's own exegetical argument attached:

> `ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ⲁⲛⲕ ⲟⲩⲛⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲛ̅ⲣⲉϥⲕⲱϩ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲙⲛ̅ ⲕⲉⲛⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲛ̅ⲥⲁⲃⲗ̅ⲗⲁⲓ̈` — *I am a jealous God and there is no other God beside me*
>
> `ⲡⲁⲓ̈ ⲇⲉ ⲉϥⲧⲁⲩⲟ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟϥ˙ ⲉϥⲣ̅ⲥⲏⲙⲁⲛⲉ ⲛ̅ⲛ̅ⲁⲅⲅⲉⲗⲟⲥ ⲉⲧϣⲟⲟⲡ' ϣⲁⲣⲟϥ ϫⲉ ⲟⲩⲛ ⲕⲉ ⲛⲟⲩⲧⲉ ϣⲟⲟⲡ ⲉⲛⲉ ⲙ̅ⲛ̅ ⲕⲉⲟⲩⲁ ⲅⲁⲣ ϣⲟⲟⲡ' ⲛⲉ ⲛⲓⲙ ⲡⲉⲧϥⲛⲁⲕⲱϩ ⲉⲣⲟϥ`
>
> *In saying this he indicated to the angels with him that another God exists; for if no other existed, of whom would he be jealous?* — fol. 13:8–13

**[OBSERVATION]** This is a formal argument from the letter of Exodus, of the kind a commentator makes. It is not myth-making. It reasons from a scriptural predicate to a conclusion about what the verse concedes.

## 11. The text states the naming law itself — twice

This is the most important thing in the tractate for the corpus-wide reading recorded in [findings/SYNTHESIS_the_whole_picture.md](findings/SYNTHESIS_the_whole_picture.md), because it removes the need to infer the law from behaviour. The tractate says it outright.

**First (fol. 12:26–33).** Of the seven powers:

> `ⲛⲁⲓ̈ ⲙⲉⲛ ⲁⲩϯ ⲣⲁⲛ ⲉⲣⲟⲟ̣ⲩ ⲕⲁⲧⲁ ⲡⲉⲟⲟⲩ ⲛ̅ⲛⲁⲧⲡⲉ ⲉⲡϣⲟⲣ̣[ϣⲣ̅ ⲛ̅ⲛⲓ]ϭⲟⲙ ⲛ̅ⲣⲁⲛ ⲇⲉ ⲉⲛⲧⲁⲩⲧⲁⲁⲩ ⲉⲣⲟ̣[ⲟⲩ ϩⲓⲧⲛ̅] ⲡⲟⲩⲁⲣⲭⲓⲅⲉⲛⲛⲏⲧⲱⲣ ⲉⲩⲣ̅ ϭⲟⲙ' ϩⲣⲁⲓ ⲛ̅ϩⲏⲧⲟⲩ … ϩⲱⲥⲧⲉ ⲉⲩⲛ̅ⲧⲁⲩ ⲙ̅ⲙⲁⲩ ⲛ̅ⲣⲁⲛ ⲥⲛⲁⲩ`
>
> *These were given names after the glory of those above, for the destruction of the powers; but the names given them by their own begetter have power in them … **so that they have two names.***

**[OBSERVATION]** Each of these figures carries two names, from two directions, with opposite effect. The text says so in a sentence.

**Second (fol. 24:24–26).** Of the two sons of the archon, bear-faced and cat-faced, one just and one unjust:

> `ⲛⲁⲓ̈ ⲇⲉ ⲁϥⲙⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲉⲣⲟⲟⲩ ⲛ̅ⲛⲓⲣⲁⲛ ϫⲉ ⲕⲁⲓ̈ⲛ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲁⲃⲉⲗ ⲉϥⲛⲁⲩ ⲁⲧⲉϥⲡⲁⲛⲟⲩⲣⲅⲓⲁ`
>
> *He called them by the names **Cain and Abel**, with a view to his own cunning.*

**[OBSERVATION]** The familiar scriptural names are stated by the text to be a **cover deliberately applied**, and the applier is the archon. The names in the received account are not the identities.

**[READING]** These two passages together are the tractate's own warrant for the rule that persons in this literature are read from their **relation and behaviour**, not from their names — and for the corollary that a doubled or split name is a signal. The corpus does not merely behave this way; here it explains that it does, and why.

## 12. Copying without seeing: the mechanism stated

> `ⲁϥⲧⲥⲉⲛⲟϥ ⲕⲁⲧⲁ ⲡⲓⲛⲉ ⲛ̅ⲛ̅ϣⲟⲣⲡ [ⲛ̅]ⲁⲓⲱⲛ … ⲙ̅ⲡⲥⲙⲁⲧ ⲛ̅ⲁⲧⲧⲉⲕⲟ ⲟⲩⲭ ϩⲟⲧⲓ ⲛ̅ⲧⲁϥ ϫⲉ ⲁϥⲛⲁⲩ ⲁⲛⲁⲧⲧⲉⲕⲟ ⲁⲗⲗⲁ ⲧϭⲟⲙ ⲉⲧⲛ̅ϩⲏⲧϥ ⲧⲁⲓ̈ ⲉⲛⲧⲁϥϫⲓⲧⲥ̅ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲓⲧⲛ̅ ⲧⲉϥⲙⲁⲁⲩ ⲉⲁⲥϫⲡⲟ ⲛ̅ϩⲏⲧϥ ⲙ̅ⲡⲓⲛⲉ˙ ⲙ̅ⲡⲧⲥⲉⲛⲟ`
>
> *He fashioned them after the likeness of the first aeons, in the manner of the imperishable — **not because he had seen the imperishable ones**, but the power in him, which he had taken from his mother, produced in him the likeness of the pattern.* — fol. 13:1–5

**[OBSERVATION]** The archon reproduces a higher order he has never seen. What does the reproducing is the power received from above, operating *in* him.

**[READING]** This is influx stated with unusual precision: the pattern flows through, produces its likeness in the receiver, and the receiver — who then says "I am God" — takes the product for his own. The tractate is not describing a rival maker. It is describing a conduit that does not know it is one. That is why the diagnosis at fol. 11:21 is about *the place from which he came* and not about wickedness.

The same mechanism, again, at the animation of Adam:

> `ⲛⲓϥⲉ ⲉϩⲟⲩⲛ ϩⲙ̅ ⲡⲉϥϩⲟ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲓⲧⲛ̅ ⲡⲉⲕⲡ̅ⲛ̅ⲁ̅ ⲁⲩⲱ ϥⲛⲁⲧⲱⲱⲛ ⲛ̅ϭⲓ ⲡⲉϥⲥⲱⲙⲁ` — *Blow into his face from your spirit, and his body will arise.*
>
> `ⲁϥⲛⲓϥⲉ ⲉϩⲟⲩⲛ ⲉϩⲣⲁϥ ⲙ̅ⲡⲉϥⲡ̅ⲛ̅ⲁ̅ ⲉⲧⲉ ⲧⲁⲓ̈ ⲧⲉ ⲧϭⲟⲙ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉϥⲙⲁⲁⲩ ⲙ̅ⲡⲉϥⲙ̅ⲙⲉ ϫⲉ ϥϣⲟⲟⲡ ϩⲛ̅ ⲟⲩⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧ̅ⲁⲧⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ`
>
> *He blew into him his spirit — **which is the power of his mother** — he did not know, for he exists in ignorance.* — fol. 19:23–28

**[OBSERVATION]** He gives away what was never his, believing it is his. The five luminaries engineer this deliberately (`ⲁⲩϫⲓ ϣⲟϫⲛⲉ ⲛⲁϥ`, they counselled him, fol. 19:21). Genesis 2:7 is retained and re-explained.

## 13. The image in the water — three occurrences, one circuit

This motif is not decorative. It occurs at the beginning, the middle and the end of the tractate, and the Coptic marks each with a different degree-term.

**(1) Above — fol. 4:20–26.** The Father gazes into `ⲧⲡⲏⲅⲏ ⲙ̅ⲡⲙⲟⲟⲩ ⲛ̅ⲱⲛϩ` (the spring of living water) and beholds **`ⲧⲉϥϩⲓⲕⲱⲛ`** — his image. That beholding *is* Barbelo, `ⲑⲓⲕⲱⲛ ⲙ̅ⲡⲓⲁⲧⲛⲁⲩ ⲉⲣⲟϥ`, the first power.

**(2) Below — fol. 14:33–15:8.** The underside of the waters is lit from above by the appearing of the image; and:

> `ⲁⲩⲛⲁⲩ ϩⲣⲁⲓ̈ ϩⲙ̅ ⲡⲙⲟⲟⲩ ⲁⲡⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ ⲛ̅ⲧϩⲓⲕⲱⲛ` — *they saw in the water the **`ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ` of the `ϩⲓⲕⲱⲛ`***
>
> `ⲁⲙⲏⲉⲓⲛⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲛ̅ⲧⲁⲙⲓⲟ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲣⲱⲙⲉ ⲕⲁⲧⲁ ⲑⲓⲕⲱⲛ ⲙ̅ⲡⲛⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲕⲁⲧⲁ ⲡⲛ̅ⲉⲓⲛⲉ ϫⲉⲕⲁⲁⲥ ⲁⲣⲉⲧⲉϥϩⲓⲕⲱⲛ ⲛⲁϣⲱⲡⲉ ⲛⲁⲛ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ`
>
> *Come, let us make a man according to the image of God and according to our likeness, **so that his image may become a light for us***

**[OBSERVATION]** Genesis 1:26 is quoted, and the motive is supplied: to obtain light. Note also the register split inside the quotation — `ϩⲓⲕⲱⲛ` (Greek) for what is God's, `ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` (native) for what is theirs. And what they see is not the image but the **`ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ` of** the image: an imprint of a reflection.

**(3) At the return — fol. 31:23–25.** The Pronoia seals the awakened one:

> `ⲁⲉⲓⲥⲫⲣⲁⲅⲓⲍⲉ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟϥ ϩⲛ̅ ⲡⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ⲙ̅ⲡⲙⲟⲟⲩ ϩⲛ̅ ϯⲉ ⲛ̅ⲥⲫⲣⲁⲅⲓⲥ ϫⲉⲕⲁⲁⲥ ⲛ̅ⲛⲉⲡⲙⲟⲩ ϭⲛ̅ϭⲁⲙ ⲉⲣⲟϥ`
>
> *I sealed him in **the light of the water** with five seals, that death might not have power over him.*

**[READING]** One image, three waters: the spring above, the chaos below, and the light of the water at the end. The same motif carries the generation of the first emanation, the counterfeiting of the human form, and the restoration. The opposite sense is not an interpretive overlay applied to this text; it is the text's own compositional spine. This is also the closest point of contact with the Manichaean "copy in the water" material already noted in the synthesis — but note the caution recorded there: the Kephalaia is shared substrate, not independent confirmation.

## 14. The anthropogony as a correspondence map

### 14.1 The seven powers become the seven substances

Folio 12:14–25 assigns a power to each of the seven heaven-rulers. Folio 15:14–23 has those same seven powers, in the same order, each make one substance of the body:

| # | Power | Substance made |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `ⲧⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧ̅ⲭⲣⲏⲥⲧⲟⲥ` goodness | `ⲟⲩⲯⲩⲭⲏ ⲛ̅ⲕⲁⲥ` a **bone**-soul |
| 2 | `ⲧⲡⲣⲟⲛⲟⲓⲁ` forethought | `ⲟⲩⲯⲩⲭⲏ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲩⲧ'` a **sinew**-soul |
| 3 | `ⲧⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧ̅ⲛⲟⲩⲧⲉ` divinity | `ⲟⲩⲯⲩⲭⲏ ⲛ̅ⲥⲁⲣⲝ` a **flesh**-soul |
| 4 | `ⲧⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧ̅ϫⲟⲉⲓⲥ` lordship | `ⲟⲩⲯⲩⲭⲏ ⲛ̅ⲁⲧⲕⲁⲥ` a **marrow**-soul |
| 5 | `ⲧⲙⲛ̅ⲧⲉⲣⲟ` kingdom | `ⲟⲩⲯⲩⲭⲏ ⲛ̅ⲥⲛⲟϥ` a **blood**-soul |
| 6 | `ⲡⲕⲱϩ` envy | `ⲟⲩⲯⲩⲭⲏ ⲛ̅ϣⲁⲁⲣⲉ` a **skin**-soul |
| 7 | `ⲧⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧ̅ⲣⲙⲛ̅ϩⲏⲧ` understanding | `ⲟⲩⲯⲩⲭⲏ ⲛ̅ϥⲟⲩϩⲉ` a **hair**-soul |

**[OBSERVATION]** The seven rulers of the seven heavens and the seven substances of the human body are the same seven, one to one, in order. Folio 12 leaves the third power unnamed; folio 15 supplies it. The body is the heavens in ultimates, and the text builds the correspondence explicitly rather than asserting it.

**[OBSERVATION — register]** `ⲯⲩⲭⲏ` is Greek throughout; six of the seven substances are native Egyptian (`ⲕⲁⲥ`, `ⲙⲟⲩⲧ`, `ⲁⲧⲕⲁⲥ`, `ⲥⲛⲟϥ`, `ⲯⲁⲁⲣⲉ`→`ϣⲁⲁⲣⲉ`, `ϥⲟⲩϩⲉ`). The one exception is **flesh**, which takes the Greek `ⲥⲁⲣⲝ` — the theologically loaded term, and the only one that carries a polemical charge elsewhere in the corpus. I note this; I do not build on it.

**[READING — tentative]** `ⲁⲧⲕⲁⲥ` (marrow) is transparently `ⲁⲧ-` privative + `ⲕⲁⲥ` (bone) — "the boneless within the bone". If that is the live sense for the composer, then substances 1 and 4 are a matched pair: bone from goodness, and what is inside bone from lordship. Flagged as tentative; it depends on a lexical judgement I cannot settle from this text alone.

**[OBSERVATION — the sixth power does double duty]** `ⲕⲱϩ` is both the sixth power that makes the **skin**-soul (fol. 15:21) and the verb for what the powers do when they see the animated Adam shine: `ⲁⲩⲕⲱϩ ϩⲛ̅ ⲧⲟⲩⲛⲟⲩ ⲉⲧⲙ̅ⲙⲁⲩ` — *they envied him in that hour* (fol. 19:34). One word for the affection and for the substance it produces.

**[READING]** Skin is the outermost boundary, the surface at which one body ends and another begins. It is made by the affection that resents another's light. The correspondence is not asserted, it is constructed: the text names the power, names what it makes, and then shows the power operating.

### 14.2 The three hundred and sixty-five, and the right/left pairing

Folios 15:29–17:29 give a body-part-by-body-part list, each with a named angel: head, brain, eyes, ears, nose, lips, teeth, molars, throat, neck, shoulders, elbows, hands, fingers, fingernails, breasts, belly, side/rib, bones, stomach, `ⲙ̅ⲫⲏⲧ` (**heart**), lungs, liver, spleen, bowels, kidneys, sinews, spine, veins, arteries, `ⲛ̅ⲛⲓϥⲉ ⲉⲧϩⲛ̅ ⲙⲙⲉⲗⲟⲥ ⲧⲏⲣⲟⲩ` (the breaths in all the members), `ⲛ̅ⲧⲥⲁⲣⲝ ⲧⲏⲣ̅ⲥ̅` (all the flesh), thighs, knees, shins, feet, toes, toenails.

**[OBSERVATION]** Every paired member is assigned **two** angels, right and left, marked `ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲛⲁⲙ` / `ⲛ̅ϭⲃⲟⲩⲣ`, without exception and without variation across roughly seventy entries.

**[OBSERVATION]** The list then rises from anatomy to faculty (fol. 17:32–18:2): those set over `ⲛ̅ⲁⲓⲥⲑⲏⲥⲓⲥ` (the senses), over `ⲧⲁⲛⲁⲗⲏⲙⲯⲓⲥ` (reception), over `ⲧⲫⲁⲛⲧⲁⲥⲓⲁ` (imagination), over `ⲡϫⲱⲛϥ` (composition/consent), over `ⲧϩⲟⲣⲙⲏ` (impulse).

**[READING]** This is the reason the enumeration lists in this literature should not be dismissed as filler. The list is ordered — outer members, inner organs, breath, flesh, then the faculties — and it is bilaterally systematic. A scribe padding a text does not sustain a right/left distinction over seventy entries.

### 14.3 Four qualities, four passions

> `ⲧⲡⲏⲅⲏ ⲇⲉ ⲛ̅ⲛⲓⲇⲁⲓⲙⲱⲛ ⲛⲁⲓ̈ ⲉⲧϩⲛ̅ ⲡⲥⲱⲙⲁ ⲧⲏⲣϥ ⲥⲉⲧⲏϣ ⲉϥⲧⲟⲟⲩ ⲟⲩϩⲙ̅ⲙⲉ ⲟⲩⲁⲣⲟϣ ⲟⲩϩⲟϭⲃⲉⲥ ⲟⲩϣⲟⲟⲩⲉ ⲧⲟⲩⲙⲁⲁⲩ ⲇⲉ ⲧⲏⲣⲟⲩ ⲧⲉ ⲧϩⲩⲗⲏ`
>
> *The source of the demons in the whole body is determined to be four: **heat, cold, wetness, dryness**; and the mother of them all is matter.* — fol. 18:2–6

Then four ruling passions with their derivative trees (fol. 18:15–31): `ⲧϩⲏⲇⲟⲛⲏ` pleasure, `ⲧⲉⲡⲓⲑⲩⲙⲉⲓⲁ` desire, `ⲧⲗⲩⲡⲏ` grief, `ⲧⲛⲱϩⲉ` fear — from grief come envy, jealousy, pain, trouble, hard-heartedness, anxiety, mourning; from pleasure, much wickedness and empty boasting; from desire, anger, wrath, bitterness, `ⲟⲩⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧ̅ⲁ̣ⲧ̣ⲥⲉⲓ` (unsatedness); from fear, terror, fawning, agony, shame. Their mother is `ⲉⲥ̅ⲑ̅ⲏ̅ⲛ̅ⲥ̅ⲓ̅ⲥ̅ⲟⲩⲭ ⲉⲡ̅ⲓ̅ⲡ̅ⲧ̅ⲟ̅ⲏ̅` — a name built on αἴσθησις, sense-perception.

**[OBSERVATION]** The four elemental qualities of ancient physics and the four passions of ancient psychology are laid over each other in the same body, with matter as the mother of one set and sense-perception as the mother of the other.

### 14.4 The Zoroaster citation

> `ⲉϣⲡⲉ ⲕⲟⲩⲱϣⲉ ⲇⲉ ⲁⲙ̅ⲙⲉ ⲉⲣⲟⲟⲩ ϥⲥⲏϩ ϩⲣⲁⲓ̈ ϩⲙ̅ ⲡϫⲱⲱⲙⲉ ⲛ̅ⲍⲱⲣⲟⲁⲥⲧⲣⲟⲥ`
>
> *If you wish to know them, **it is written in the book of Zoroaster**.* — fol. 19:8–10

**[OBSERVATION]** The formula is a citation formula — `ϥⲥⲏϩ`, "it is written", the same construction the corpus uses for scripture — and the object is a named book (`ⲡϫⲱⲱⲙⲉ`, native word for book) attributed to Zoroaster. The tractate declines to reproduce the remainder of the list and refers the reader elsewhere, as a commentator refers to a source he expects to be available.

**[READING]** Whatever the actual document was, the composer treats the anatomical-correspondence material as belonging to an identifiable written tradition outside his own text, and outside the Genesis material he is arguing with. This is the tractate's own account of where its body-map comes from.

## 15. Three degrees of body, made in three stages

The tractate builds the human downward through three distinct constructions, and does not conflate them:

1. **The `ⲯⲩⲭⲓⲕⲏ` moulding** (fol. 15:9–19:14) — `ⲁϥⲧⲁⲙⲓⲟ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩϩⲩⲡⲟⲥⲧⲁⲥⲓⲥ ⲕⲁⲧⲁ ⲡⲓⲛⲉ ⲙ̅ⲡϣⲟⲣⲡ' ⲛ̅ⲣⲱⲙⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉⲗⲉⲓⲟⲥ`, a hypostasis after the likeness of the perfect First Man, built from the seven powers, the 365 angels, the four qualities. It lies `ⲛ̅ⲁⲣⲅⲟⲛ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲛ̅ⲁⲧⲕⲓⲙ` — idle and motionless — until the breath.
2. **The animation** (fol. 19:23–33) — the archon's breath, which is the mother's power passing through him unrecognised. The body moves, becomes powerful, and **shines**.
3. **The material remoulding** (fol. 21:5–14) — after Adam is cast down:

> `ⲉⲩⲛⲁⲡⲗⲁⲥⲥⲉ ⲛ̅ⲕⲉⲥⲟⲡ' ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲙ̅ ⲡⲕⲁϩ ⲙⲛ̅ ⲡⲙⲟⲟⲩ ⲙⲛ̅ ⲧⲕⲱϩⲧ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲡⲉⲡ̅ⲛ̅ⲁ̅ ⲡⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲛ̅ ⲧϩⲩⲗⲏ … ⲉⲧⲉ ⲡⲁⲓ̈ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲥⲡⲏⲗⲁⲓⲟⲛ ⲛ̅ⲧⲁⲛⲁⲡⲗⲁⲥⲓⲥ ⲙ̅ⲡⲥⲱⲙⲁ ⲡⲁⲓ̈ ⲉⲛⲧⲁⲩⲧⲁⲁϥ ϩⲓ ⲡⲣⲱⲙⲉ ⲛ̅ϭⲓ ⲛ̅ⲗⲏⲥⲧⲏⲥ ⲧⲙⲣ̅ⲣⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲃ̅ϣⲉ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲡⲁⲓ̈ ⲁϥϣⲱⲡⲉ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲣⲱⲙⲉ ⲉϣⲁϥⲙⲟⲩ`
>
> *moulded again out of earth, water, fire and the spirit that is from matter … this is **the cave of the remoulding of the body**, which the **robbers** (`ⲗⲏⲥⲧⲏⲥ`) put on the man, **the bond of forgetfulness**; and he became a mortal man.*

**[OBSERVATION]** Three constructions, three vocabularies, one after another. The tractate is explicit that the second body is a later imposition on a body that already existed.

## 16. Correspondence performed as method: the tree read part by part

Folio 21:19–35 reads the archons' paradise item by item, giving each natural feature its interior value:

> their luxury is **bitter** · their beauty is **lawless** · their luxury is **deception** · their trees are **impiety** · their fruit is **incurable poison** · their promise is **death**

and then their tree, member by member:

> `ⲉⲣⲉ ⲧⲉϥⲛⲟⲩⲛⲉ ⲥⲁϣⲉ` — its **root** is bitter
> `ⲛⲉϥⲕⲗⲁⲇⲟⲥ ϩⲉⲛⲙⲟⲩ ⲛⲉ` — its **branches** are death
> `ⲧⲉϥϩⲟⲓ̈ⲃⲉⲥ ⲟⲩⲙⲟⲥⲧⲉ ⲡⲉ` — its **shadow** is hate
> `ⲟⲩⲁⲡⲁⲧⲏ ⲧⲉⲧϣⲟⲟⲡ ϩⲛ̅ ⲛⲉϥϭⲱⲱⲃⲉ` — deception is in its **leaves**
> `ⲡⲉϥϯⲟⲩⲱ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲧⲱϩ̅ⲥ̅ ⲛ̅ⲧⲡⲟⲛⲏⲣⲓⲁ` — its **bloom** is the **anointing** of wickedness
> `ⲡⲉϥⲕⲁⲣⲡⲟⲥ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲙⲟⲩ` — its **fruit** is death
> `ⲟⲩⲉⲡⲓⲑⲩⲙⲓⲁ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲉϥⲥⲡⲉⲣⲙⲁ` — its **seed** is desire

**[OBSERVATION]** This is a complete part-by-part correspondence of a single natural object, structurally identical to the body-part list — root, branch, shadow, leaf, bloom, fruit, seed, each with its interior term.

**[OBSERVATION — the anointing inverted]** `ⲡⲧⲱϩ̅ⲥ̅` here is the same word as at fol. 6:23, where `ⲁϥⲧⲱϩⲥ̅ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟϥ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲛ̅ ϯⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧⲭⲣ̅ⲥ̅` — *he anointed him with his `ⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧ̅ⲭⲣ̅ⲥ̅`* — makes the Autogenes `ⲡⲉⲭⲣ̅ⲥ̅`, "until he became perfect, **lacking nothing** of `ⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧ̅ⲭⲣ̅ⲥ̅`". One anointing produces the Christ by reception; the other is the bloom of the tree of death. Same verb, opposite sense.

**[READING]** The Christ-title in this tractate is earned by being anointed, not possessed by nature — `ϣⲁⲛⲧⲉϥ ϣⲱⲡⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉⲗⲉⲓⲟⲥ ⲉⲛϥϣⲁⲁⲧ ⲛ̅ⲗⲁⲁⲩ ⲁⲛ`, *until he became perfect, lacking nothing*. That is reception language, and it lines up with Philip's *"he merely accepted (the gift)"* rather than with the substitute Christologies of VII,2 and VII,3. Recorded in the synthesis backlog as part of the passion split; this is a further witness on the participation side.

## 17. "Not as Moses said" — three times, plus an argument from Isaiah

**[OBSERVATION]** The tractate corrects the literal sense of Genesis three times, with the same formula, and each time supplies what it takes to be the actual referent:

| Passage | The letter denied | The correction |
|---|---|---|
| fol. 13:19–21 | `ⲙ̅ⲡⲣ̅ⲙⲉⲉⲩⲉ ϫⲉ ⲕⲁⲧⲁ ⲑⲉ ⲉⲛⲧⲁϥϫⲟⲟⲥ ⲛ̅ϭⲓ ⲙⲱⲩ̈ⲥⲏⲥ ϩⲓϫⲛ̅ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲩⲉⲓⲟⲟⲩⲉ` — *not as Moses said, "above the waters"* (Gen 1:2) | the mother moving over the deficiency; `ⲡⲕⲓⲙ ⲇⲉ ⲡⲉ ⲡϣⲉⲉⲓ` — *the moving is the going to and fro* |
| fol. 23:3–4 | `ⲕⲁⲧⲁ ⲑⲉ ⲁⲛ ⲉⲛⲧⲁ{{ϥ}}ϫⲟⲟⲥ ⲛ̅ϭⲓ ⲙⲱⲩ̈ⲥⲏⲥ ϫⲉ ⲧⲉϥⲃⲉⲧⲥⲡⲓⲣ` — *not as Moses said, "his rib-bone"* (Gen 2:21) | `ⲁϥⲉⲓⲛⲉ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲙⲉⲣⲟⲥ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉ ⲧⲉϥϭⲟⲙ` — *he brought out a portion of his power* |
| fol. 29:6–8 | `ⲕⲁⲧⲁ ⲑⲉ ⲁⲛ ⲉⲛⲧⲁⲙⲱⲩ̈ⲥⲏⲥ ϫⲟⲟⲥ ϫⲉ ⲁⲩϩⲱⲡ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲟⲩ ϩⲛ̅ ⲟⲩⲕⲓⲃⲱⲧⲟⲥ` — *not as Moses said, "they hid in an ark"* (Gen 7) | `ⲁⲩϩⲱⲡ' ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲟⲩ ϩⲛ̅ ⲟⲩⲕⲗⲟⲟⲗⲉ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ` — *they hid in a **luminous cloud*** |

And at fol. 22:22–28 the method is defended by a further scriptural citation:

> `ⲁϥϫⲟⲟⲥ ⲅⲁⲣ ϩⲣⲁⲓ̈ ϩⲙ̅ ⲡⲉϥϣⲟⲣⲡ ⲛ̅ϫⲱⲙⲉ ϫⲉ ⲁϥϫⲧⲟ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟϥ ⲁⲗⲗⲁ ϩⲛ̅ ⲛⲉϥⲁⲓⲥⲑⲏⲥⲓⲥ ⲕⲁⲓ ⲅⲁⲣ ⲁϥϫⲟⲟⲥ ϩⲓⲧⲛ̅ ⲡⲉⲡⲣⲟⲫⲏⲧⲏⲥ ϫⲉ ϯⲛⲁϩⲣⲟϣ ⲉϩⲣⲁⲓ̈ ⲁϫⲛ̅ ⲛⲟⲩϩⲉⲧⲉ ϫⲉⲕⲁⲁⲥ ⲛ̅ⲛⲟⲩϯ ϩⲧⲏⲩ ⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲛ̅ⲛⲟⲩⲛⲁⲩ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ`
>
> *For he said in his first book, "He made him sleep" — **but it was in his perceptions**. For he said through the prophet, "I will make heavy their hearts, that they may not attend and may not see."* (Isa 6:10)

**[OBSERVATION]** The tractate argues that "sleep" in Genesis 2:21 means insensibility, and proves it from Isaiah, where God himself is said to dull perception. **This is exegesis of shared scripture, using one text to fix the sense of another.** It is not the construction of a rival mythology; the composer takes it for granted that his reader accepts both books and can be moved by the argument.

## 18. The rib, and what could not be caught

> `ⲁϥⲟⲩⲱϣⲉ ⲛ̅ϭⲓ ⲡⲣⲱⲧⲁⲣⲭⲱⲛ ⲁⲉⲓⲛⲉ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲥ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲙ̅ ⲡⲉϥ ⲥⲡⲓⲣ ⲧⲉⲡⲓⲛⲟⲓⲁ ⲇⲉ ⲙ̅ⲡⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ⲟⲩⲁⲧⲧⲉϩⲟⲥ ⲧⲉ ⲉϥⲡⲏⲧ ⲛ̅ⲥⲱⲥ ⲛ̅ϭⲓ ⲡⲕⲁⲕⲉ ⲙ̅ⲡ̅ϥⲧⲉϩⲟⲥ`
>
> *The first archon wanted to bring her out of his side. But **the Epinoia of light is ungraspable** (`ⲟⲩⲁⲧⲧⲉϩⲟⲥ`) — the darkness pursued her and did not catch her.* — fol. 22:29–32

He gets instead `ⲟⲩⲙⲉⲣⲟⲥ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉ ⲧⲉϥϭⲟⲙ` — a portion of Adam's power — and forms it `ϩⲛ̅ ⲟⲩⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ ⲛ̅ⲥϩⲓⲙⲉ ⲕⲁⲧⲁ ⲡⲓⲛⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉⲡⲓⲛⲟⲓⲁ` — in a female `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ` after the `ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` of the Epinoia.

**[OBSERVATION]** The outward shape is a copy of what could not be seized. The `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`/`ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` pair does the work again.

Then the awakening:

> `ⲁⲥϭⲱⲗⲡ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ' ⲙ̅ⲡⲕⲁⲗⲩⲙⲙⲁ ⲉⲧⲛ̅ϩⲣⲁⲓ̈ ϩⲓϫⲛ̅ ⲡⲉϥϩⲏⲧ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲁϥⲣ̅ⲛⲏⲫⲉ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲙ̅ ⲡϯϩⲉ ⲙ̅ⲡⲕⲁⲕⲉ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲁϥⲥⲟⲩⲛ̅ ⲧⲉϥⲉⲓⲛⲉ`
>
> *She uncovered the **veil** (`ⲕⲁⲗⲩⲙⲙⲁ`) that was over his **heart**; and he became sober from the **drunkenness** of darkness; and he recognised his own `ⲉⲓⲛⲉ`.* — fol. 23:6–9

**[OBSERVATION]** `ⲕⲁⲗⲩⲙⲙⲁ` over the heart is the vocabulary of 2 Corinthians 3:15. What is recognised is his `ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` — the interior likeness, not the outward shape.

## 19. Shadow, and the way back

> `ⲁϥⲟⲩⲱⲛϩ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲛ̅ϭⲓ ⲡⲣⲱⲙⲉ ⲉⲧⲃⲉ ⲧϩⲁⲓ̈ⲃⲉⲥ ⲙ̅ⲡⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ⲧⲁⲓ̈ ⲉⲧϣⲟⲟⲡ ϩⲣⲁⲓ̈ ⲛ̅ϩⲏⲧϥ`
>
> *The man became manifest because of **the shadow of the light** which is in him.* — fol. 20:28–30

**[READING]** Shadow here is not privation. It is what makes an interior light visible from outside — the trace by which the presence of the light is inferred. That is a compact statement of how a natural effect discloses its interior cause.

And the Epinoia's office (fol. 20:19–24):

> `ⲉⲥⲣ̅ϩⲩⲡⲟⲩⲣⲅⲉⲓ ⲛ̅ⲧⲕⲧⲓⲥⲓⲥ ⲧⲏⲣⲥ ⲉⲥϣⲉⲡ ϩⲓⲥⲉ ⲛⲙ̅ⲙⲁϥ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲉⲥⲥⲱϩⲉ ⲙ̅ⲙⲁϥ ⲉϩⲟⲩⲛ ⲁⲡⲉϥⲡⲗⲏⲣⲱⲙⲁ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲉⲥⲧⲥⲉⲃⲟ ⲙ̅ⲙⲁϥ ⲁⲧⲉϥϭⲓⲛⲉⲓ ⲁⲡⲓⲧⲛ̅ ⲙ̅ⲡⲥⲡⲉⲣⲙⲁ ⲉⲥⲧⲥⲁⲃⲟ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟϥ ⲉⲡⲙⲁⲓ̈ⲧ ⲃ̅ⲃⲱⲕ ⲉϩⲣⲁⲓ̈ ⲡⲙⲁⲓ̈ⲧ' ⲉⲛⲧⲁϥⲉⲓ ⲉϩⲣⲁⲓ̈ ⲙ̅ⲙⲁⲩ`
>
> *serving the whole creation, **suffering with him**, restoring him to his fullness, teaching him about the descent of the seed, teaching him **the way of ascent — the way by which he came down***

**[OBSERVATION]** The path up is stated to be the path down, traversed the other way. The causal direction is not reversed — influx still comes from above throughout — but the itinerary is the same one.

**[OBSERVATION]** `ⲉⲥϣⲉⲡ ϩⲓⲥⲉ ⲛⲙ̅ⲙⲁϥ`, *suffering with him*, is participation language, and it recurs at fol. 28:1 of the Spirit: `ⲉⲧϣⲡϩⲓⲥⲉ ⲛⲙ̅ⲙⲏⲧⲛ̅`, *who suffers with you*. Compare *Treatise on the Resurrection*: "We suffered with him, and we arose with him." Another witness on the participation side of the passion split.

## 20. The eschatology: states, not verdicts

The closing catechism (fol. 25:16–27:31) sorts souls, and the sorting is by **condition**, not by sentence.

**[OBSERVATION]** Four outcomes:

1. Those on whom the Spirit of life descends and who join with the power — `ⲥⲉⲛⲁⲟⲩϫⲁⲉⲓ˙ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲛ̅ⲥⲉϣⲱⲡⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉⲗⲉⲓⲟⲥ`, saved and made perfect, purified `ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲓⲧⲛ̅ ⲕⲁⲕⲓⲁ ⲛⲓⲙ`.
2. Those on whom the power came but who did not do the works — `ⲡⲁⲛⲧⲏ ⲡⲁⲛⲧⲱⲥ ⲥⲉⲛⲁⲟⲩϫⲁⲓ̈`, *by all means they will be saved*, and transferred. The reason given is general: `ⲧⲇⲩⲛⲁⲙⲓⲥ ⲅⲁⲣ ⲛⲁⲉⲓ ⲉϩⲣⲁⲓ̈ ⲁϫⲛ̅ ⲣⲱⲙⲉ ⲛⲓⲙ ⲁϫⲛⲧ̅ⲥ̅ ⲅⲁⲣ ⲙⲛ̅ ϭⲟⲙ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉⲗⲁⲁⲩ ⲁϩⲉ ⲉⲣⲁⲧ̅ϥ̅` — *the power comes upon **every** man; without it none could stand.*
3. Those under the counterfeit spirit — put into another body until they wake:

> `ϣⲁⲩⲧⲁⲁⲥ ⲉⲧⲟⲟⲧⲟⲩ ⲛ̅ⲛⲉⲝⲟⲩⲥⲓⲁ … ⲁⲩⲱ ϣⲁⲩⲕⲱⲧⲉ ⲛⲙ̅ⲙⲁⲥ ϣⲁⲛⲧⲥⲛⲉϩⲥⲉ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲓⲧⲛ̅ ⲧⲃ̅ϣⲉ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲛ̅ⲥϫⲓ ⲉⲣⲟⲥ ⲙ̅ⲡⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲧⲁⲓ̈ ⲧⲉ ⲑⲉ ⲉⲥϣⲁⲛϫⲱⲕ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϣⲁⲥⲟⲩϫⲁⲓ̈`
>
> *they go around with it until it awakes from forgetfulness and receives knowledge; and thus, when it is completed, it is saved* — fol. 27:8–11

And the return ends: the soul is joined to one in whom the Spirit of life is, and `ⲉϣⲁⲩⲛⲟⲩϫⲉ ϭⲉ ⲁⲛ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲥ ⲉϩⲟⲩⲛ ⲉⲕⲉⲥⲁⲣⲝ` — *she is not cast into another flesh again* (fol. 27:20–21). **Return is remedial and terminating, not a standing condition.** John asks how this works and the Saviour **rejoices** (`ⲁϥⲣⲁϣⲉ`) at the question — the only time in the tractate.

4. The single irreversible case: those who **knew and turned away** — taken where `ⲙⲛ̅ ⲙⲉⲧⲁⲛⲟⲓⲁ ϣⲟⲟⲡ ⲙ̅ⲙⲁⲩ`, *there is no repentance there*; `ⲛ̅ⲛⲉⲧⲁϩϫⲉ ⲟⲩⲁ ⲁⲡⲉⲡ̅ⲛ̅ⲁ̅`, those who blasphemed the Spirit (fol. 27:22–31).

**[READING]** The one unrecoverable state is not ignorance and not embodiment. It is having received and then repudiated. Everything else is a delay. Note the alignment with the synoptic saying about the sin against the Spirit — the tractate is not inventing a category here either.

**[OBSERVATION — flesh as worn]** `ⲧϩⲩⲡⲟⲥⲧⲁⲥⲓⲥ ⲟⲩⲁⲁⲧⲥ̅ ⲛ̅ⲧⲥⲁⲣⲝ ⲧⲁⲓ̈ ⲉⲧⲟⲩⲫⲟⲣⲉⲓ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲥ` — *only the substance of the flesh which they **wear*** (φορεῖν, fol. 25:34–35). The body is a garment in the ordinary correspondential sense, and the verb says so.

## 21. Fate as a manufactured bond

> `ⲁⲩⲣ̅ⲛⲟⲉⲓⲕ ⲁⲧⲥⲟⲫⲓⲁ ⲛ̅ⲛⲟⲩⲉⲣⲏⲩ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲁⲩϫⲡⲟ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲓⲧⲟⲟⲧⲟⲩ ⲁⲩⲥⲁϣ ⲛ̅ⲧϣⲓⲙⲁⲣⲙⲉⲛⲏ ⲉⲧⲉ ⲧⲁⲓ̈ ⲧⲉ ⲧϩⲁⲏ ⲙ̅ⲙⲣ̅ⲣⲉ ⲉⲧϣⲃ̅ⲃⲓⲁⲉⲓⲧ`
>
> *and they begot Heimarmene, **the last of the changeable bonds*** — fol. 28:13–16
>
> `ⲁⲩⲙⲟⲩⲣ ⲅⲁⲣ ⲛ̅ϩⲛ̅ϣⲓ ⲙⲛ̅ ϩⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓϣ ⲙⲛ̅ ϩⲛ̅ⲕⲁⲓⲣⲟⲥ` — *for they bound with **measures and times and seasons*** — fol. 28:30–31

**[OBSERVATION]** Astral determinism is not treated as a feature of the cosmos but as a product, and it is called `ϣⲃ̅ⲃⲓⲁⲉⲓⲧ` — the same root as the "counterfeit" spirit and as the archon's deviant `ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ` at fol. 10:8. What binds by measure and time is in the same category as what imitates.

## 22. The Pronoia hymn: three descents

The tractate closes with a first-person hymn (fol. 30:11–31:25) in three movements, each marked by a counted descent:

> `ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ϭⲉ ⲧⲉⲡⲣⲟⲛⲟⲓⲁ ⲉⲧϫⲏⲕ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲛ̅ⲧⲉ ⲡⲧⲏⲣϥ … ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲣ̅ ⲡⲙⲉⲉⲩⲉ ⲙ̅ⲡⲉⲡⲗⲏⲣⲱⲙⲁ` — *I am the perfect Pronoia of the all … I am the **remembrance** of the Pleroma*

1. **First** — `ⲁⲉⲓⲙⲟⲟϣⲉ ϩⲛ̅ ⲧⲙⲛ̅ⲧ̅ⲛⲟϭ ⲙ̅ⲡⲕⲁⲕⲉ … ⲁⲉⲓϩⲟⲡⲧ ⲉⲣⲟⲟⲩ ⲉⲧⲃⲉ ⲧⲟⲩⲕⲁⲕⲓⲁ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲙ̅ⲡⲟⲩⲥⲟⲩⲱⲛⲧ` — *I walked into the greatness of the darkness … I hid myself from them because of their wickedness, and they did not know me.*
2. **Second** — the foundations of chaos shake; she withdraws `ⲉⲧⲁⲛⲟⲩⲛⲉ ⲛ̅ⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ϫⲉⲕⲁⲁⲥ ⲛ̅ⲛⲟⲩⲧⲉⲕⲟ ⲙ̅ⲙⲟⲟⲩ ϩⲁⲑⲏ ⲙ̅ⲡⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓϣ` — *to my root of light, lest they be destroyed before the time.*
3. **Third** — `ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ⲉⲧϣⲟⲟⲡ ϩⲙ̅ ⲡⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ … ⲁⲉⲓⲃⲱⲕ ⲉϩⲟⲩⲛ ⲉⲧⲙⲏⲧⲉ ⲙ̅ⲡⲟⲩϣⲧⲉⲕⲟ **ⲉⲧⲉ ⲡⲁⲓ̈ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲉϣⲧⲉⲕⲟ ⲡⲥⲱⲙⲁ**` — *I am the light that exists in the light … I entered the midst of their prison, **which is the prison of the body***

Then the call and the response:

> `ⲡⲉⲧⲥⲱⲧⲙ̅ ⲧⲱⲟⲩⲛ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ϩⲙ̅ ⲫⲓⲛⲏⲃ ⲉⲧϩⲟⲣϣ` — *He who hears, **arise from the heavy sleep***
>
> `ⲛⲓⲙ ⲡⲉⲧⲙⲟⲩⲧⲉ ⲙ̅ⲡⲁⲣⲁⲛ` — *Who is it that calls my name?*
>
> `ⲧⲱⲟⲩⲛⲕ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲛ̅ⲕⲣ̅ ⲡⲙⲉⲉⲩⲉ … ⲛ̅ⲕⲟⲩϩⲁⲕ ⲁⲧⲉⲕⲛⲟⲩⲛⲉ ⲉⲧⲉ ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ⲡⲉ ⲡϣⲁⲛϩⲧⲏϥ` — *Arise and **remember** … follow your **root**, which is I, the merciful one*

**[OBSERVATION]** Three descents, each deeper, each named; the third reaches the body. The awakening is `ⲣ̅ ⲡⲙⲉⲉⲩⲉ` — remembering — and the speaker is `ⲡⲣ̅ ⲡⲙⲉⲉⲩⲉ ⲛ̅ⲧⲡⲣⲟⲛⲟⲓⲁ`, the remembrance itself. The one who calls is what is remembered.

**[READING]** The three descents correspond to the three degrees at which the tractate has been operating throughout — the aeonic, the psychic moulding, the material body. The Pronoia does not simply descend once to a bottom; she descends *through the same three levels the anthropogony built downward*, in reverse.

## 23. Where the reading strains

Recorded honestly.

1. **The Pentad is mixed register.** `ⲡⲙⲉⲉⲩⲉ` (thought), `ⲧⲙ̅ⲛ̅ⲧ̅ⲁⲧⲧⲉⲕⲟ` (incorruption), `ⲡⲱⲛϩ ϣⲁ ⲉⲛⲉϩ` (eternal life) and `ⲧⲙⲉ` (truth) are native; `ⲧⲡⲣⲟⲅⲛⲱⲥⲓⲥ` is Greek. So is the whole luminary apparatus. **The native/Greek split of §3 does not cleanly map onto interior/exterior everywhere.** It holds where the text sets up a doublet deliberately (`ⲅⲛⲱⲥⲓⲥ`/`ⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ`, `ⲭⲁⲣⲓⲥ`/`ϩⲙⲟⲧ`, `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ`/`ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`) and it does not hold as a general rule of the vocabulary. Do not over-extend it.
2. **The `ⲥⲁⲣⲝ` anomaly.** Six of seven body substances are native; flesh is Greek. I have no explanation that is not speculation.
3. **The angel-name lists are unreconstructable.** Many of the 365 names carry heavy diacritics and several are damaged. Nothing here depends on any individual name.
4. **`ⲁⲧⲕⲁⲥ` for marrow** is flagged tentative above.
5. **The two accounts of Sophia's restoration differ in emphasis.** At fol. 14:10–12 she is raised above her son to the ninth until her deficiency is corrected; at fol. 23:21–24 she is `ⲧⲛ̅ⲥⲱⲛⲉ ⲧⲥⲟⲫⲓⲁ` who *came down in innocence* to correct her deficiency, and is identified with `ⲍⲱⲏ`, the mother of the living. Whether these are one movement described twice or a seam between sources, I cannot tell from the Coptic.
6. **The descent is not commissioned.** Unlike the Manichaean pattern, Sophia here acts on her own initiative and the whole subsequent structure is repair. This remains a real divergence from that witness, as already recorded in the synthesis. It is not a variant of one story.

## 24. Summary of the teaching

Stated as compactly as the text allows:

1. There is one reality, named at different degrees; the Coptic marks the degree by register (§3), and marks the difference between a constant interior and its variable presentations by `ⲥⲙⲁⲧ`/`ⲉⲓⲛⲉ` against `ⲙⲟⲣⲫⲏ`/`ⲧⲩⲡⲟⲥ` (§1).
2. The order of production is **will, then word, then work** (§4). Everything that comes into being does so by being asked for, looked at, revealed, established, and returning glory (§5).
3. The disorder is that sequence inverted: thought proceeding without the will it should follow (§7). What results is not a rival substance but a **sickness** (§9) — a being who receives everything, knows nothing of his source, and says "I am God" (§10, §12).
4. The human form is at the top, not the bottom (§6). What is below is an imprint of an imprint of it, taken from a reflection in water (§13).
5. The body is that reflection worked out in ultimates: seven heaven-ruling powers becoming seven substances, one to one, in order (§14.1); every member and every faculty assigned, bilaterally, without exception (§14.2); the elemental qualities and the passions laid over each other in the same frame (§14.3). The composer refers this material to a named written source (§14.4).
6. Reading a natural thing correctly means reading it part by part against its interior — which the text demonstrates on a tree (§16) as it did on a body.
7. The letter of scripture is not discarded but corrected, three times by the same formula and once by argument from another prophet (§17). Names in the received account can be covers deliberately applied, and the text says so (§11).
8. Return is by remembering; the way up is the way down (§19, §22). Nothing is finally lost except what has been received and then repudiated (§20).
