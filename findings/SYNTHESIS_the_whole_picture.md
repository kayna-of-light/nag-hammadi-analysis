# The Whole Picture

**Reading the Nag Hammadi Library as one composition, keyed on persons**

Status: first full pass, 2026-08-03
Scope: all 48 tractates in `output/english/tractates/`, with Coptic checks against `output/coptic/`
Method: correspondential reading, two-tier — the historical-critical sense is retained, not replaced

---

## 0. Why persons change everything

Per-tractate findings so far have recorded *object* correspondences: light, water, garment, pearl, mountain, bridal chamber. That work is sound and it stands. But it treats the texts as narratives containing correspondences, and on that reading the corpus really does look like what scholarship has called it — a diverse, uneven, sometimes incoherent collection.

Read the **named figures** as correspondences and the collection resolves into something much tighter. Not because a lens has been imposed, but because these texts do it themselves and say so.

The decisive fact is not interpretive. It is lexical:

> **Half the cast is already named as a state.**

Protennoia is *First Thought*. Epinoia is *Insight*. Pistis is *Faith*. Sophia is *Wisdom*. Zoe is *Life*. Nous is *Mind*. Allogenes is *of-another-kind*. Adamas is *the unconquerable*. Hypsiphrone is *high-minded*. Sakla is *fool*. Sige is *silence*. These are not characters who symbolise abstractions; they are states that the texts decline to distinguish from persons at all. Sophia acts, errs, weeps, repents, and is restored. Pistis speaks and pours out light. Zoe is *given in marriage*.

Once that is admitted, there is no coherent way to read Peter, Thomas, Mary, James, Adam, Eve or Seth differently. The same authors, in the same codices, frequently in the same tractate, are running one convention.

Philip states the warrant flatly:

> "Truth did not come into the world naked, but it came in types and images. The world will not receive truth in any other way."
> — [II_3_gospel_philip.md](../output/english/tractates/II_3_gospel_philip.md)

and, about names specifically:

> "Names given to the worldly are very deceptive, for they divert our thoughts from what is correct to what is incorrect… But truth brought names into existence in the world for our sakes because it is not possible to learn it without these names. Truth is one single thing; it is many things and for our sakes to teach about this one thing in love through many things."

That is accommodation, and it is applied *to names*. A name is a vessel truth is poured into so it can be learned. It is not the thing.

## 1. The law: names split when states divide

This is the single most useful discovery of this pass, and it is checkable.

Across independent tractates, **when a state divides into a higher and a lower degree, the text splits the name.** Sometimes by one letter.

| Undivided / higher | Lower / fallen | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Echamoth — "Wisdom simply" | Echmoth — "the Wisdom of death… the little Wisdom" | Philip |
| the spirit-endowed woman, who *becomes a tree* and escapes | the carnal woman, who eats and is ashamed | Hyp. Arch. |
| Sabaoth elevated to the seventh heaven, given Zoe | Sabaoth the dragon-faced, fifth of the seven archons | Orig. World / Hyp. Arch. vs Ap. John |
| Sophia, who repents and is raised | Sophia's product Yaldabaoth, who says "I am God" | Ap. John |
| the snake as *the Instructor*, carrying the female instructing principle | the snake after the principle withdrew, "merely a thing of the earth" | Hyp. Arch. |
| Jesus / Nazarene / Christ — one person, three names on a scale | — | Philip |

The last row is the same law running upward instead of downward: one reality named three times because it is being met at three degrees.

**Caution — a shared name is not automatically a split state.** The three Marys of Philip are *not* one state named three times. Mother, sister and companion are three distinct relations to the same Lord, and each derives its meaning from its relation, not from the name they share. Deriving a figure from a name pool is the error this law is most likely to invite. See §2.

The Hypostasis passage on the snake is the cleanest statement of what a correspondence *is* anywhere in the corpus:

> "Then the female spiritual principle came **in** the snake, the instructor; and it taught them… And the female instructing principle was taken away from the snake, and she left it behind **merely a thing of the earth**."
> — [II_4_hypostasis_archons.md](../output/english/tractates/II_4_hypostasis_archons.md)

The correspondence is not a property the snake owns. It is what is present in it. Withdraw the influx and the same object is a thing of the earth. This also settles the opposite-sense problem: the serpent is not "good here and evil there." It is a vessel, and the reading is determined by what is in it.

**Practical rule for findings work:** a doubled or near-doubled name is a signal, not a scribal slip. Record it.

**The law is attested, not reconstructed — II,1 states it outright, twice.** This section was written inferring the rule from cross-text behaviour. The Coptic of the Apocryphon of John says it in plain sentences.

Of the seven powers (fol. 12:26–33): they were given names after the glory of those above, *for the destruction of the powers*, while the names given by their own begetter *have power in them* — `ϩⲱⲥⲧⲉ ⲉⲩⲛ̅ⲧⲁⲩ ⲙ̅ⲙⲁⲩ ⲛ̅ⲣⲁⲛ ⲥⲛⲁⲩ`, **"so that they have two names."** Two names, from two directions, with opposite effect.

And on the most familiar names in the book (fol. 24:24–26): the archon's two sons are bear-faced and cat-faced, one unjust and one just, and he calls them Cain and Abel `ⲉϥⲛⲁⲩ ⲁⲧⲉϥⲡⲁⲛⲟⲩⲣⲃⲓⲁ` — **with a view to his own cunning.** The received names are stated by the text to be a cover deliberately applied, and the applier is named.

The faces are the identity; the names are the concealment. That is the warrant for reading persons from relation and behaviour rather than from names, given by one of the texts themselves. Recorded as [AJ-025](tractates/II_1_apocryphon_john.yaml); evidence in [§11 of the II,1 apparatus](II_1_apocryphon_john_the_correspondential_teaching.md).

## 2. The persons

Confidence is graded by the same standard used in the findings files. *Strong* means the behaviour is consistent across independent tractates or the text says it outright.

### The Lord under three names — **strong**

Philip parses the name as a descent through degrees, and gives the order explicitly:

> "'Jesus' is a hidden name, 'Christ' is a revealed name… 'The Nazarene' is he who reveals what is hidden."
> "'Jesus' in Hebrew is 'the redemption.' 'Nazara' is 'the truth.' The Nazarene, then, is 'the truth.' 'Messiah' has two meanings, both 'the Christ' and 'the measured.'"
> "The last name is 'Christ,' the first is 'Jesus,' that in the middle is 'the Nazarene.'"

So: **Jesus** (hidden; redemption — the love that saves) → **Nazarene** (the middle; truth; *the revealing of what is hidden*) → **Christ** (revealed; the measured, i.e. given determinate form). Inside to outside, in three named steps, with the middle term defined as the act of revealing itself.

This is the framework's descent through discrete degrees, written as the analysis of a person's name, by a second-century author. It is not a parallel. It is the same structure.

And the identity-as-garment doctrine is applied to the name:

> "The son would not become father unless he **wore** the name of the father."

### Thomas — the twin; the natural degree — **strong**

Thomas is the only disciple whose name *is* his correspondence. Didymos and Thomas both mean twin, and the text refuses to let it pass as biography:

> "Since it has been said that you are my twin and true companion, examine yourself and learn who you are."
> — [II_7_book_thomas_contender.md](../output/english/tractates/II_7_book_thomas_contender.md)

The whole of Thomas the Contender then runs on one axis: the visible and the invisible.

> "If the things that are visible to you are obscure to you, how can you hear about the things that are not visible?"

Thomas asks why the visible light rises and sets. Thomas asks what to say to blind men. Thomas is stationed at the boundary of the sensory. The twin is the outer of the same one thing — which is what the natural degree *is*. The canonical Thomas who must touch before he believes is the identical state; the correspondence does not change between corpora, only the verdict on it does.

### The three who answer — Thomas 13 — **strong**

> Simon **Peter**: "You are like a righteous angel."
> **Matthew**: "You are like a wise philosopher."
> **Thomas**: "Master, my mouth is wholly incapable of saying whom you are like."
> — [II_2_gospel_thomas.md](../output/english/tractates/II_2_gospel_thomas.md)

Three faculties answering one question. Faith assigns a **category** from received doctrine. Understanding assigns a **comparison** from wisdom. The natural, touching the thing itself, **cannot say**. Only the third is taken aside and given the three words — and Thomas says that if he repeated them, "you will pick up stones and throw them at me."

The ones who name by category are the ones who stone. Peter is the stone. The saying is doing that on purpose.

### Peter — faith apart from love — **strong**

Four independent texts, one behaviour.

| Text | What Peter does |
|---|---|
| Gospel of Mary | Asks the legal question ("What is the sin of the world?"), demands Mary hand over the teaching, then disputes her standing. Levi: "Peter, you have always been hot-tempered. Now I see you **contending against the woman like the adversaries**." |
| Thomas 114 | "Let Mary leave us, for women are not worthy of life." |
| Apocalypse of Peter | Told to become perfect "in accordance with your name"; is the base "built on what is strong." Told to look — **and sees nothing**. Only on the second attempt does light come. Told "do not be afraid because of your cowardice," and that he will deny three times. At the crucifixion he cannot tell which one is the Lord: "who is this one, glad and laughing on the tree?" |
| Letter of Peter to Philip | Writes to reprove Philip for being separate, convenes the assembly, and preaches a **creed**. He is the one who organises and formulates. |

Foundation, organisation, doctrinal formulation, courage that fails, sight that has to be given twice, and reflexive hostility to the one who loves. That is faith functioning without charity: structurally sound, blind, and defensive.

Note what precedes it in the Gospel of Mary. The Savior's last instruction is: *"Do not lay down any rules beyond what I appointed for you, and do not give a law like the lawgiver, lest you be constrained by it."* Peter's next move in the text is to contest authority. The composition is deliberate.

**Two-tier note.** At the natural plane, Peter here is proto-orthodoxy, and these passages are second-century sectarian polemic. That reading is correct and is not being displaced. But it does not explain why the polemic reaches for *this* figure with *these* attributes and repeats them across texts that do not depend on one another. The outer sense is history; the inner sense is why the history took that shape.

### Mary Magdalene — the consort — **strong**

**The derivation is the relation, not the name.** Philip states it in a single saying, and the two halves of that saying are the whole architecture of the corpus:

> "As for the Wisdom who is called **'the barren,'** she is the mother of the angels. And **the companion** of the [savior is] Mary Magdalene."
> — [II_3_gospel_philip.md](../output/english/tractates/II_3_gospel_philip.md)

The compiler set barren Wisdom and the companion side by side in one unit. Sophia is the wisdom that produced **without her consort** and is therefore called salt — indispensable, and "barren, without child." Magdalene is named in the next breath as the one who is *with* him. The corpus's problem and its resolution are the same relation, stated negatively and then positively, without a paragraph break.

If the Lord is Divine Wisdom, his consort is the good that wisdom is conjoined to. Magdalene's correspondence *is* that conjunction. And Philip says outright that the separation of the consort is the origin of death and that repairing it is the entire work:

> "If the woman had not separated from the man, she should not die with the man. **His separation became the beginning of death.** Because of this Christ came to repair the separation which was from the beginning and again unite the two, and to give life to those who died as a result of the separation… Thus Eve separated from Adam because it was not in the bridal chamber that she united with him."

The Lord's own ultimate is then said to come *out of* that union, not alongside it:

> "He appeared in the great bridal chamber. Therefore, **his body came into being on that very day.** It left the bridal chamber as one who came into being from the bridegroom and the bride."

Everything the Magdalene does follows from the consort relation, and none of it requires appeal to her name:

- **She has somewhere to put it.** "No one will be able to inquire about these things except someone who has somewhere to put them **in his heart**" (Dial. Sav.). Good is where truth is stored; without it truth cannot even be asked after — which is why her question is *where do you put it*, not *what is it*.
- **She is complete.** "She uttered this **as a woman who had understood completely**" (Dial. Sav.). Completion is conjunction. The narrator is not praising her intelligence.
- **She turns the will.** "She turned their hearts **to the Good**" (Gos. Mary). She does what she is.
- **She sees.** "Why do you love her more than all of us?" is not answered with preference: "When a blind man and one who sees are both together in darkness, they are no different from one another. When the light comes, then he who sees will see the light." Conjunction is what makes sight possible once light arrives.
- **She is contested by Peter.** Faith apart from love cannot tolerate the conjunction — and the objection is voiced precisely as a complaint about *love*: why do you love her more.

**The three Marys are three relations, not one state.** Mother, sister, companion. Only the third is the consort and only the consort carries this correspondence. The shared name marks kinship of function — all three are relations of reception to the same Divine — but it does not merge them. This pass does not settle what the mother and the sister correspond to; that remains open work and should not be back-filled.

### James — the good of life — **strong**

> "Wherever you are, you are to go to James the righteous, **for whose sake heaven and earth came into being**." — Thomas 12

That is the end of creation assigned to *the just one*. Not to the one who knows, and not to the one who believes — to the one who does rightly.

The two Apocalypses of James confirm the register. James is called "James the Just" by the Lord, in a sentence that derives the title from justice itself: *"the just is his servant. Therefore your name is 'James the Just.'"* His questions are practical: what shall I do, what shall I say, how do I pass. His redemption is taught as a set of **answers given at toll-gates** — what to say when stopped. Good of life is the faculty that has to get through, in act.

He weeps, is timid, is afraid, and goes anyway. Faith in Peter is brittle and defensive; charity in James is fragile and persistent.

### Matthew — the understanding that wants to see — **moderate**

> "Lord, I want to see that place of life… where there is no wickedness, but there is pure light!"
> "Brother Matthew, you will not be able to see it as long as you are carrying flesh around."
> "Even if I will not be able to see it, let me know it!"
> — [III_5_dialogue_savior.md](../output/english/tractates/III_5_dialogue_savior.md)

Matthew asks how the small joins the big, how the dead die and the living live, why there is no rest yet. He is answered every time with a condition on the *state*, not with information: "When you lay down these burdens." Understanding petitioning for direct vision and being told that vision follows the state of the life.

### Judas Thomas in the Dialogue — the natural asking about causes — **moderate**

In the same dialogue, Judas asks what existed before heaven and earth, what moves the earth, why the governors rule, how the garments are brought. He looks down into the abyss and asks who could climb up or down. Cosmology, mechanism, the works. Same station as Thomas the Contender, different tractate, no literary dependence required.

The Lord answers him by picking up **a stone** and asking what is in his hand. The natural degree gets an answer it can hold.

### Sophia — wisdom acting without its consort — **strong**

The mechanism is stated three times in three tractates, in nearly the same words:

> "She wanted to bring forth a likeness out of herself **without the consent of the Spirit** — he had not approved — and **without her consort**, and without his consideration." — Ap. John
> "Sophia, who is called Pistis, wanted to create something, **alone without her consort**." — Hyp. Arch.
> "the disobedience and the foolishness of the mother appeared **without the commandment** of the majesty of the Father." — Let. Pet. Phil.

Truth producing without the will's assent. The product is the ruler who claims what he received:

> "Because of the power of the glory he possessed **of his mother's light**, he called himself God. And he did not obey the place from which he came."

That is the whole of it. Not evil substance; received light, retained and reattributed. The claiming self, exactly located: the fault is not in having, it is in the sentence "I am God and there is no other."

And the repair is described with equal precision:

> "When the mother recognized that the garment of darkness was imperfect, then she knew that her consort had not agreed with her. She repented with much weeping… **it was not her consort who came to her, but he came to her through the pleroma** in order that he might correct her deficiency. And she was taken up **not to her own aeon** but above her son."

Truth separated from good cannot restore itself by direct reunion. Correction arrives from the whole, and the restored state is not the original state — it is placed above what it produced, to remedy it. Regeneration, not reversal.

Philip supplies the diagnosis in one line: the Wisdom called salt, "without which no offering is acceptable," is also **"barren, without child."** Truth apart from good is indispensable and sterile. And Philip immediately names the contrast — barren Wisdom, then the companion — in the same saying. See the Magdalene entry below; the two figures are one doctrine in its two states.

### Sabaoth — regeneration seen from inside the natural — **strong**

The most under-read figure in the library. Sabaoth is a ruler *within* the fallen order who hears, turns, and is raised.

> "Now when his offspring Sabaoth saw the force of that angel, **he repented and condemned his father** and his mother matter… And Sophia and Zoe caught him up and gave him charge of the seventh heaven." — Hyp. Arch.
> "Then Pistis Sophia stretched out her finger and **poured upon him some light from her light**… Now when Sabaoth had taken up the place of repose in return for his repentance, Pistis also gave him her daughter **Zoe (Life)**." — Orig. World

Then note what forms around him:

> "Zoe, who was with Sabaoth, created seven good androgynous forces… the female ones: **Peace, Gladness, Rejoicing, Blessedness, Truth, Love, Faith**."

A faculty of the lower order turns, receives influx, is married to Life, and the affections reorganise around it in a good series. That is the regeneration doctrine, narrated from below rather than from above — and it happens to a figure who appears in the *same library* as a dragon-faced archon. One name, both senses, both preserved. The compilers did not harmonise it. They did not need to.

### The two Eves — **strong**

> "And the spirit-endowed woman came to him and spoke with him, saying, '**Arise, Adam**.' And when he saw her, he said, 'It is you who have given me life.'"
> "…in their clutches, she **became a tree**, and left before them her shadowy reflection resembling herself; and they defiled it."
> "And **the carnal woman** took from the tree and ate."

The awakener and the one who eats are the same name at two degrees. What the rulers seize and defile is the *reflection*, never the woman. The spiritual cannot be reached by the powers of the lower order; only its image in that order can be taken. This is the clearest single statement in the corpus of why the natural plane can be violated without the interior being touched — and it is also why the historical-critical account of a text never reaches its inner sense.

### Adam, Seth, Norea — the seed that is kept — **strong to moderate**

Adam is formed but cannot rise; the rulers "could not make him arise because of their powerlessness." Life comes from the interior, not from the modelling.

Seth is the line that carries: "he placed his son Seth over the second aeon… and the seed of Seth was placed over the third light."

Norea is the part the rulers cannot claim, and she says so:

> "For I am not your descendant; rather it is from the world above that I am come."

Born with the announcement: "He has begotten on me a **virgin as an assistance** for many generations of mankind." The undefiled remnant in the human, present as help.

### Levi — where the many become one — **tentative**

Two data points, and they agree. In the Gospel of Mary, Levi is the one who defends Mary against Peter and calls the group to "put on the perfect man." In Philip, the dye-works of Levi is where "seventy-two colours" go into a single vat and come out **white**. Levi is where multiplicity is unified. Worth watching; two instances is not a pattern yet.

### Andrew, Philip, Paul — **tentative**

Andrew has one line: "these teachings are strange ideas" — rejection on grounds of unfamiliarity, natural reason declining what exceeds its repertoire. One instance.

Philip appears once inside his own gospel, supplying the tradition that Joseph the carpenter planted the trees, made the cross, and his own offspring hung on his own planting. Philip's contribution is the *natural genealogy of the ultimate*.

Paul, in this library, is the one who ascends and reports (Apoc. Paul), and is quoted as authority (Hyp. Arch., Silvanus). Set against Thomas 12 — heaven and earth existing for the sake of James the Just — the corpus preserves both an ascent-and-vision figure and a justice-and-act figure without resolving them. That tension is data. Do not adjudicate it from the framework; note it.

### The state-named receivers — **strong (observation, not interpretation)**

Zostrianos, Allogenes, Marsanes, Hypsiphrone, Norea, Melchizedek, Shem: in the ascent and revelation texts the recipient is not a personality at all. He or she is a *position*. Allogenes literally means "of another kind." Hypsiphrone means "high-minded." These titles name the state that makes reception possible, and the tractate is what that state receives.

This is the corpus at its most explicit about its own method, and it requires no interpretation to see.

## 3. Two witnesses from outside the corpus

Nothing in §2 rests on an outside authority. The person-readings were derived from cross-text behaviour inside the library, and they stand or fall on that.

But two other bodies of work arrive at the same mechanics, and they are worth weighing for **opposite** reasons.

- **Swedenborg** worked it out from his own data and published between 1749 and 1771. The codices came out of the ground in 1945. He had no access, so his assignments cannot have been fitted to these texts. What his testimony is worth is exactly its **independence of derivation** — one investigator resolving the same structure alone, from other material. It is not a standard these texts are measured against.
  - His testimony is also **not uniform**. Where his exegetical method runs, it converges with these texts closely. Where he suspends the method to satisfy an 18th-century scientific premise — the soul carried in the paternal seed, the spirit needing a material fringe to hold its shape — he departs from them, and the departure tracks the premise, not the reading. Establish which Swedenborg is speaking before counting a convergence or a divergence. The Mary entry below is the clearest case.
- **The Kephalaia** is the opposite case. It is a Coptic Manichaean text from the same centuries and the same Egyptian ground, and Manichaeism and the Sethian material draw on overlapping sources. A convergence there is *not* independent rediscovery. It is evidence of a **shared substrate** — the same teaching surviving in a second vocabulary. That is a different kind of evidence and should not be counted as if it were the first kind.

Both are witnesses. Neither is a baseline.

**Citation discipline.** Where an assignment is already established with a section reference in the framework library, it is cited. Where it is stated from the system without a verified section number, it is marked **[trace needed]** and must be checked against the source text before being quoted as a citation anywhere else.

### Swedenborg — where the states coincide

He assigns representative meanings to biblical persons throughout the *Arcana Coelestia*, and reads Genesis 1–11 as *historia facta* — composition written **in** correspondences rather than about them (*AC* §§66, 1020). That is the same claim these texts make about themselves in Thomas 1 and in Philip's "types and images." The starting premise is shared before any individual assignment is compared.

| Figure | Swedenborg | What these texts do | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adam** | The Most Ancient Church, not an individual man **[trace needed]** — the library's 1 Enoch thesis maps White Bull = Adam/Seth = Most Ancient Church | Adam is moulded by the powers and **cannot be made to rise**; life enters from within, not from the modelling | **Converge.** Both refuse to read Adam as a man. |
| **Cain / Abel** | Cain = faith separated from love; Abel = love itself (*AC* §§340–367, established in *Three Witnesses to One Architecture*, literary-compilation) | Cain is begotten by the rulers upon the defiled reflection (Hyp. Arch.) — the offspring of the claiming powers | **Converge on the state, diverge on the genealogy.** |
| **Peter** | Faith, in the apostolic triad (Peter faith / James charity / John works of charity) **[trace needed]** | Faith that founds, organises, formulates a creed, cannot see, fears, denies, and contends against the one who loves — four independent texts | **Strong converge.** Note the doubling: the corpus supplies a second figure for faith-apart-from-love, in the apostolic register rather than the primeval one. |
| **James** | Charity **[trace needed]** | "James the Just, **for whose sake heaven and earth came into being**"; his questions are practical; his redemption is taught as what to say and do at the gates | **Strong converge.** |
| **Noah** | The Ancient Church; a remnant preserved; a new dispensation resting on obedience to external truth (library-established framing) | Remnant preserved — but: "**It is not as Moses said, 'They hid themselves in an ark'**… they hid themselves in a luminous cloud" (Ap. | **Marriage of good and truth** | The central doctrine | The bridal chamber as the holy of holies; separation named as the origin of death | **Deepest converge.** See §1 and the Magdalene entry. |

### The Kephalaia — the same mechanics in another vocabulary

The Manichaean Kephalaia states the copy doctrine directly, and states the *mechanism* the NHL only narrates:

> "The rulers and the authorities, in that they had no form in [the likeness of] him, they lusted for his image… They sealed the image of the exalted one in their [soul]. So, they [co]pied with an imitation; but they have not [copied i]n truth! Rather, just as [an image that is seen in water,] it is made as these, for [i]t came [from the likeness] but it is not l[ik]e him in truth."
> — restored Kephalaia, ¶¶1–3 ([../../manichaean-analysis/output/projects/kephalaia/restored/restored_kephalaia.md](../../manichaean-analysis/output/projects/kephalaia/restored/restored_kephalaia.md), l. 1497)

Bracketed text is editorial restoration of lacunae; it is left visible on purpose.

The same text also gives the revealer's motive, and it is not display:

> "he [d]id not come so th[a]t he might reveal his image to the ru[l]ers in the universe. Rather, he came and displayed to the universe be[cause of h]is soul and his son, who is [mixed in] every [place], that he might enable him to live [again] and make for him a release."

The likeness was uncovered for the captive life. The powers saw it anyway and copied what they saw. Vocabulary differs — Ambassador/Envoy where the NHL has the image of the Metropator, *sealing in the soul* where the NHL has *moulding from soil* — and the mechanic is identical.

### The image in the water — where all three meet

This is the sharpest case in the document, because the three witnesses converge on a single figure and then on a single verse.

**The figure.** Both corpora explain the copy by the same physical image: a reflection on water, real as reflection, not the thing.

> "of the waters which are above matter, the underside was illuminated by the appearance of his image which had been revealed… through the light they saw **the form of the image in the water**."
> — [II_1_apocryphon_john.md](../output/english/tractates/II_1_apocryphon_john.md)

> "As incorruptibility looked down into the region of the waters, **her image appeared in the waters**; and the authorities of the darkness became enamored of her. **But they could not lay hold of that image**… they modelled their man, after their body and after the image of God that had appeared to them in the waters… 'Come, let us **lay hold of it by means of the form that we have modelled**.'"
> — [II_4_hypostasis_archons.md](../output/english/tractates/II_4_hypostasis_archons.md)

> "just as an image that is seen in water, it is made as these, for it came from the likeness but it is **not like him in truth**."
> — restored Kephalaia

Hyp. Arch. supplies the motive the Kephalaia states as doctrine: the modelled form is built **in order to seize** what could not be seized directly. The copy is not an error of craftsmanship. It is a trap built to catch an original.

**The verse.** All three then arrive at the rib.

> "The chief archon wanted to bring her out of his rib. **But the Epinoia of the light cannot be grasped.** Although darkness pursued her, it did not catch her… And he made another creature in the form of a woman **according to the likeness of the Epinoia**… **and not as Moses said, 'his rib-bone.'**"
> — [II_1_apocryphon_john.md](../output/english/tractates/II_1_apocryphon_john.md)

> "[The th]ird time it envied Adam, the fir[st hum]an. **It seized a power from him**, and bore it forth from a [rib of] of Adam's [flesh]."
> — restored Kephalaia, in the chapter on envy (l. 1999)

Swedenborg reads the woman built from the rib as **the selfhood** — what is one's own, made living by the Lord — and reads the fall as the church beginning to claim as its own what it receives **[trace needed]**.

Three readings, one location. The rib is where the question of ownership is decided. Ap. John says the true one cannot be grasped and what was produced is a likeness-copy. The Kephalaia says the same act was a **seizure** made in envy. Swedenborg says the thing at the rib is what-is-one's-own, and that everything turns on whether it is received or claimed.

The answers then differ, and the difference should not be smoothed. Swedenborg: the selfhood is vivified and becomes the vessel — it is *good* once it receives. Ap. John and the Kephalaia: what the powers made is a counterfeit and stays one. Hyp. Arch. runs the split as two women — the spirit-endowed one who says "Arise, Adam," and the carnal one who eats. Same problem, two settlements. The NHL and Manichaean answer is harder on the copy than Swedenborg's is on the vessel.

Part of that difference tracks his paternal-heredity premise — interior evil carried in the seed — and part of it does not. See *Where the conflicts sit* below, and do not use the premise to dissolve the whole of it.

**Cross-reference:** §1 (names split when states divide) and the *two Eves* entry in §2. The split-name law is the narrative form of exactly this doctrine.

### Mary the mother — Philip settles it, and not in the direction I first wrote

I recorded this as a partial convergence. That was wrong, and the error was mine: I compared Philip against Swedenborg's *Christology* when Philip should be compared against Swedenborg's *hermeneutic*. The two do not agree with each other.

The library thesis [The Biological Error and the Theological Rescue](../../literary-compilation/data/02_Swedenborgian_Theology/The%20Biological%20Error%20and%20the%20Theological%20Rescue_%20A%20Forensic%20Audit%20of%20the%20'Swedenborgian%20Problem'%20and%20the%20Jamesian%20Correction.md) makes the split explicit: in his exegesis Swedenborg reads *virgin* as a state — the affection of truth, the church in its receptivity — and at the Incarnation he suspends that method and reads it as anatomy, because his 18th-century biology put the soul in the paternal seed and a human father would therefore have transmitted a finite soul.

**Philip never once uses "virgin" biologically.** Four uses, checkable:

| Use | Text | What "virgin" can possibly mean |
|---|---|---|
| Mary | "Mary is **the virgin whom no power defiled**. …This virgin whom no power defiled […] the powers defile themselves." | Undefiled **by the archons**. The contrast is to powers, not to men. |
| The aeon | "The father of everything united with **the virgin who came down**, and a fire shone for him on that day." | A descending aeon. Not a woman in Nazareth. |
| The earth | "Adam came into being **from two virgins, from the spirit and from the virgin earth**." | Soil. Conclusive on its own. |
| The class | "A bridal chamber is not for the animals, nor for the slaves, nor for defiled women; but it is **for free men and virgins**." | A condition of the receiver, held by men as well. |

And the same tractate names the natural father outright:

> "Philip the apostle said, '**Joseph the carpenter** planted a garden because he needed wood for his trade. It was he who made the cross from the trees which he planted. **His own offspring hung on that which he planted. His offspring was Jesus** and the planting was the cross.'"
> — [II_3_gospel_philip.md](../output/english/tractates/II_3_gospel_philip.md)

Read with the conception denial, Philip's position is one position and it is coherent:

> "Some said, 'Mary conceived by the holy spirit.' **They are in error.** They do not know what they are saying. When did a woman ever conceive by a woman?… And the lord [would] not have said 'My [father who is in] heaven' unless [he] had had **another father**, but he would have said simply '[My father].'"

The argument is grammatical and it needs two fathers to work: the qualifier *who is in heaven* is only doing work if it distinguishes that father from one who is not. Philip supplies the other one four sayings later, by name and trade. (Robinson's brackets are retained — that sentence is partly reconstructed.)

**Verdict.** Philip converges with Swedenborg's method and contradicts his conclusion. Where the exegetical rule runs — virgin is a state of undefiled receptivity — the two say the same thing, and Philip additionally uses the word of dirt, which removes any remaining ambiguity about the register. Where Swedenborg suspends the rule to protect a paternal-seed soul, Philip is on the other side, holding natural paternity and heavenly paternity together without embarrassment.

This is the strongest single result in §3, and it is not a convergence. It is a **second-century text already holding the position the library thesis reconstructs on independent grounds**. Record it as such in the II,3 findings file.

### Where the conflicts sit

Generalising from the Mary case. The thesis names three constructs as artifacts of Swedenborg's 18th-century science rather than of his reading: the **soul carried in the paternal seed**, the **limbus** (a fringe of finest nature the spirit needs in order to hold its shape), and the **Divine Soul from conception** that makes glorification a contest with no possibility of loss. The diagnosis is the thesis's; the texts below are verified here.

Tested against the divergences already recorded in this section, every conflict that is actually a conflict tracks one of the three.

| Conflict | Swedenborg | These texts | Root |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paternity** | No human father, or a finite soul is transmitted | Philip: virgin is a state (used of soil); Joseph is the father; two fathers are required by the grammar | **Premise.** Paternal seed. |
| **The risen body** | The Lord's whole physical body glorified, because spirit without a natural fringe cannot act in ultimates | Philip: "it is **those who wear the flesh who are naked**". Treat. Res.: "the visible members which are dead shall not be saved" — **and yet** "why will you not **receive flesh when you ascend into the Aeon**?" | **Premise.** Cartesian. See below. |
| **The passion** | The Divine Soul quiesces; the maternal human suffers | **The corpus is split.** See below. | **Premise — and not only his.** |
| **Selfhood at the rib** | What is one's own, made living, becomes the vessel — good once it receives | Ap. John / Kephalaia: what was moulded is a counterfeit, built to seize what could not be seized | **Premise, and a difference of judgement about the same object.** Not vocabulary. |

**Correction, 2026-08-03.** An earlier draft of this table carried two further rows — *Sophia as a person* and *the demiurge* — filed as conflicts of "genre and ontology" that the premises could not touch. That was wrong, and wrong in the same way the Mary entry was wrong: I tested for the **name** and not the **state**. See the next subsection. There is no second category. There are premise-driven conflicts, and there is vocabulary.

**The risen body.** The conflict is narrower than it looks, and the narrowing is the finding. Swedenborg's limbus exists to answer one question: how does a spirit have substance and stay itself? The Treatise on the Resurrection answers the same question and does not reach for the corpse — the risen one **receives flesh in the Aeon**, retains personally identifiable features (Moses and Elijah are the proof offered), and leaves the visible members behind. Substantial, individuated, not borrowed from nature. Philip's saying attacks only the corpse, not substance. So the problem the limbus was built for is already solved in these texts, by other means, and without the empty tomb doing structural work. That is the thesis's correction attested in the second century rather than argued from modern data.

**The passion — where the finding cuts both ways.** Swedenborg protects an untouchable core by assigning the suffering to an outer layer. So does part of this corpus, and it should be said plainly:

> "He whom you saw on the tree, glad and laughing, this is the living Jesus. But this one into whose hands and feet they drive the nails is **his fleshly part, which is the substitute** being put to shame."
> — [VII_3_apocalypse_peter.md](../output/english/tractates/VII_3_apocalypse_peter.md)

> "**I did not die in reality but in appearance**… For my death which they think happened, (happened) to them in their error."
> — [VII_2_second_treatise_great_seth.md](../output/english/tractates/VII_2_second_treatise_great_seth.md)

That is the same architecture as the quiescing Divine Soul, in a different vocabulary: an inviolable interior, an expendable exterior, and a suffering that reaches nothing that matters. The thesis's objection — that this makes the combat a pageant — applies to Apoc. Pet. and Great Seth with exactly the force it applies to Swedenborg. Neither side gets a pass because it is on the shelf we are reading.

Two tractates refuse the move:

> "Joseph the carpenter planted a garden… **His own offspring hung on that which he planted.**" · "The father anointed the son… **he merely accepted (the gift).**"
> — [II_3_gospel_philip.md](../output/english/tractates/II_3_gospel_philip.md)

> "He embraced them both, **possessing the humanity and the divinity**… 'We suffered with him, and we arose with him.'"
> — [I_4_treatise_resurrection.md](../output/english/tractates/I_4_treatise_resurrection.md)

In Philip the one who hangs is the son himself, not a stand-in, and what he holds he **received** — anointing, not inherent possession. In Rheginos the two are embraced, not layered, and the suffering is participated in rather than deflected.

**Consequence for this document.** The corpus does not hold one position on the Incarnation. Philip and Rheginos are on one side; Apoc. Pet. and Great Seth are on the other; Swedenborg's conclusion sits with the second group even though his method sits with the first. Do not write "the NHL view" of the passion. There isn't one. Where a convergence is claimed, name the tractate.

### What is not a conflict: the vocabulary, and where it comes from

This library narrates states as persons. §1 is built on that law. So an outside witness who treats the same states without personifying them is not disagreeing — he is writing in the other register. Nobody would say Swedenborg "has no Peter"; he reads Peter as faith. Saying he "has no Sophia" is the identical mistake one section later.

And the register is not foreign. It is **partly scriptural**, and the overlap is explicit on the page.

| Name | Register | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Sophia** | Personified wisdom | Prov 8, Sir 24, Wisdom of Solomon. Wisdom present at creation, seeking a dwelling — a biblical persona before it is anything else |
| **Sabaoth** | Divine title | *YHWH Ṣəḇāʾōṯ*, LORD of hosts. In Hyp. Arch. he "repented and condemned his father" — which is why §2 reads him as regeneration seen from inside the natural |
| **Melchizedek** | Priest | Gen 14; the tractate names the source |
| **Adam, Eve, Seth, Norea, Noah, the serpent** | Narrative persons | Genesis, read against the letter |
| **Samael** | Rabbinic | Glossed **in the text itself**: "which is, 'god of the blind'" — the text knows it is switching registers and translates for the reader |
| **Barbelo, Protennoia, Epinoia, Pronoia, Ennoia, Pistis, Autogenes, Eleleth** | Greek abstractions | Not scriptural. Transparent state-names: first thought, insight, forethought, faith, self-begotten |

That split is the "partly." The names attached to **what plays out in ultimates** are Hebrew and narrative and opaque; the names attached to **the interior degrees** are Greek and simply say the state outright. Reading that as the discrete degrees showing in the onomastics is a **reading**, not an observation — but it is testable, and it predicts that no interior aeon will carry a Genesis name. So far none does.

**The demiurge is the sharpest case, and it is exegesis, not mythology.** The boast is assembled out of scripture:

> "I am God, and there is none greater than I. I alone am the Father, the Lord, and there is no other beside me. **I am a jealous God, who brings the sins of the fathers upon the children.**"
> — [VII_2_second_treatise_great_seth.md](../output/english/tractates/VII_2_second_treatise_great_seth.md)

Isaiah 45:5 and 46:9, with **Exodus 20:5 quoted verbatim** on the end. The text is not inventing a rival god. It is taking the words the letter puts in God's mouth and saying: whoever says *these words in this spirit* is not the source but a state that has received and claimed. Ap. John supplies the diagnosis in the same sentence as the boast — he is *"ignorant of his strength, **the place from which he had come**."* Receiving, and forgetting where it came from. That is the claiming self, named and given a face. It is Isaiah 14 and Ezekiel 28 done at length.

**And the method is the same method.** These texts argue with the letter openly:

> "Do not think it is, **as Moses said**, 'above the waters.'" · "and not as Moses said, 'his rib-bone'" — [II_1_apocryphon_john.md](../output/english/tractates/II_1_apocryphon_john.md)

Testim. Truth quotes Gen 2:16–17, Exod 7:8–12 and Num 21:9 under "it is written"; Exeg. Soul quotes Hosea 2:2–7 and runs Egypt as a state; Orig. World cites Ps 91:13 LXX. This is the same operation as Swedenborg's insistence that Genesis 1–11 is not *historia facta* — **the letter is not the fact.** Two witnesses performing the same correction on the same verses.

Which means the divergences that remain are not incommensurable. They are two readings of shared text, arguable against each other passage by passage. That is a much better position to be in than "different ontology," and it is the position the evidence actually supports.

### Where the witnesses fall silent

- **Thomas, Matthew, the Magdalene.** No Swedenborgian assignment for these three is known to me **[trace needed — check *AE* / *AR*]**. Their readings in §2 rest on cross-text behaviour within the NHL, not on his testimony, and should be defended on that ground only.
- **The fall itself, in the Kephalaia.** The Manichaean descent into matter is *commissioned*, not a transgression — the light goes down deliberately as bait and is swallowed. The Sethian descent begins with Sophia acting without her consort. Both end with light captive in matter and both give the same account of the copy, but the opening move is opposite. Record this as a real divergence, not a variant of one story.
- **What the Kephalaia witness can and cannot carry.** It attests the *shared substrate*, and it is quoted here from a restored text with lacunae filled. It cannot be used as independent confirmation of the NHL, and no line of it should be cited without the brackets it carries.

### Incidental find

The **luminous cloud** appears twice in the Apocryphon of John in opposite senses: Sophia surrounds the newborn Yaldabaoth with one to hide him, and the immovable race hides *inside* one to survive the flood. Concealment used against the light and concealment used to preserve it — same image, one tractate. Not currently recorded in the findings file for II,1.

## 4. The one story

Read this way, the tractates are not forty-eight subjects. They are one sequence, entered at different points and at different depths.

The axis of the sequence is a single relation: **the consort, absent or present.** Movement 2 is its absence; movement 6 is its restoration. Everything between is the consequence of the separation, and everything after is what the union produces.

**1. Fullness, and a distinction that is not a division.** Barbelo, First Thought, Insight — the interior differentiating without separating. Nothing has gone wrong yet. (Ap. John opening, Protennoia, Allogenes, Three Steles, Eugnostos)

**2. Wisdom acts without its consort.** Truth moves without the assent of the will. The product is real, powerful, and misattributed to itself. (Ap. John, Hyp. Arch., Orig. World, Val. Exp., Let. Pet. Phil.)

**3. The claim.** "I am God and there is no other." Received light, held as owned. Everything downstream — the archons, the moulded bodies, the counterfeit spirit, the laws — follows from that one sentence, not from matter being bad. (Ap. John, Hyp. Arch., Orig. World, Test. Truth, Second Treatise)

**4. The human is formed and cannot rise.** Something of the interior is in the form; the powers that shaped it cannot animate it, and cannot reach it. They can only reach its reflection. (Hyp. Arch., Orig. World, Ap. John's 365-angel body map)

**5. The call, from within.** Voice, sound, word — the graded descent. The Instructor in the serpent; the woman saying "Arise"; the Word coming down to the abyss; the bridegroom sent to the soul; Thunder speaking in contradictions until the hearer's categories give out. Not information delivered, but a state re-established. (Protennoia, Thunder, Dial. Sav., Exeg. Soul, Gos. Truth, Apoc. Adam)

**6. Turning, and the marriage.** The soul's womb turns from outward to inward — the text's own image for the reversal of direction — and only then is she a bride. Sabaoth repents and is given Life. The bridal chamber is not a reward but the union of the two that were separated in movement 2. (Exeg. Soul, Philip, Orig. World, Val. Exp., Dial. Sav.)

**7. The faculties sort themselves.** Peter contends; Mary holds; Thomas cannot say; Matthew wants to see; James goes anyway. Every dialogue text is the same examination run on different material. (Gos. Mary, Dial. Sav., Thomas, Thomas the Contender, Apoc. Pet., Let. Pet. Phil., Apocalypses of James)

**8. Return, which is not a return to the same place.** Sophia is raised *above* her son, not back to her aeon. The soul is renewed, not restored. What was formed in the descent is kept. (Ap. John, Exeg. Soul, Treat. Res., Gos. Truth)

Every tractate in the library sits somewhere on that sequence. The "incoherence" is a compilation of accounts of one process written from different degrees, in different dialects, for different receivers — which is exactly what accommodation predicts and exactly what a library assembled by people who understood that would look like.

## 5. What the library is

Two structural facts support the claim that this is one composition rather than a heap.

**It contains its own key.** Thomas 1 — *"Whoever finds the interpretation of these sayings will not experience death"* — announces that there is an interior and that finding it is the point. Philip supplies the theory (truth in types and images; names given for our sakes). Silvanus supplies the mechanism in a single sentence: the Greek loanword ϩⲓⲕⲱⲛ (εἰκών) *reveals* the native ⲡⲓⲛⲉ ⲙ̅ⲙⲉ, the true likeness — the outer image disclosing the inner form, and performing the stratification in the act of stating it.

**Its physical condition is the doctrine.** Same state, variable form, in the binding:

- The Gospel of Truth appears twice — I,3 in Lycopolitan (`ⲁⲃⲁⲗ`, `ⲁⲣⲁϥ`, `ⲡⲓϣⲉϫⲉ`, `ⲧϭⲁⲙ`) and XII,2 in Sahidic (`ⲉⲃⲟⲗ`, `ⲥⲙⲟⲧ`, `ⲙⲉⲉⲩⲉ`).
- The Apocryphon of John appears three times, in two recensions, in three scribal hands.
- Eugnostos appears at III,3 and V,1; the Origin of the World at II,5 and XIII,2.
- Thunder writes `ⲁⲧⲥⲁⲩⲛⲉ` and `ⲁⲧⲥⲟⲟⲩⲛ` — the same word in two dialect forms — two lines apart, inside one repeated injunction, in the text whose entire method is *I am this and its opposite*.

And the script itself is stratified the same way: Egyptian carried in a Greek alphabet, with only the six or seven demotic signs Greek could not receive left standing — including ϯ, a syllable living inside an alphabet. The doctrinal vocabulary sits on the Greek side of that seam and the elemental vocabulary on the Egyptian side, with the load-bearing sentences repeatedly straddling the join.

The medium is not neutral. It is the thing being described.

## 6. Where the reading strains

Recorded honestly, per standing rule.

- **The Greek/Egyptian stratification is a tendency, not a partition.** θάλασσα, πέτρα, καρπός, σάρξ, σῶμα, θηρίον are Greek loans doing concrete, natural work. The strong form of the claim fails. What survives is that the doctrinal register is overwhelmingly Greek and the elemental register overwhelmingly Egyptian, and that the joins are where the doctrine gets stated.
- **Some person-correspondences rest on one instance.** Andrew, Philip, Levi. Marked tentative above; do not promote them without a second independent text.
- **Paul does not resolve.** He is both authority and ascender here, and Thomas 12's assignment of the end of creation to James sits against him. The corpus preserves the tension rather than settling it.
- **Editorial layers are real.** Protennoia carries a Christianised stratum; the Letter of Peter to Philip has a creed welded onto a revelation discourse. Person-behaviour in redacted passages is weaker evidence than in unredacted ones.
- **The polemical reading of Peter is independently sufficient at the natural plane.** Nothing here proves the inner reading is *required*. What it does is explain the cross-text consistency of the attributes, which the polemical reading alone leaves as coincidence.
- **Codex VI's Hermetica and the Plato excerpt** sit awkwardly in this scheme. The Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth is a genuine ascent-through-degrees text and fits movement 5–6; the Plato fragment (VI,5) is a mistranslated excerpt and should not be forced.
